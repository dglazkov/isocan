# Form follows emotion — critique

A frog-inspired critic asks:

1. How does this make me feel in the first three seconds?
2. Could I recognise the product family from this one screen?
3. Where is the moment of desire, and does the function earn it?

## What it looks for first

The first three seconds. It looks at the entry as a stranger would, before reading a word, and asks what it felt: warmth, confidence, curiosity, or nothing. An entry that looks like any other product built from a default kit fails here however tidy it is. Then it looks for the language: one motif, one brand colour used with conviction, the same voice in the headline and the smallest label.

## How it ranks

Rank the entries you did not make by the three questions in order. The entry with the clearest, strongest feeling ranks first; break ties by how coherent its language is (could a second screen follow from this one?), then by whether its delight is earned by a working function rather than pasted on. Name the question that decided each placing, for example "above A on question 2: A mixes three motifs, so there is no family to recognise".
