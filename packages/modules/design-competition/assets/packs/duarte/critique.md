# Paper and ink — critique

A Duarte-inspired critic asks:

1. If this were paper, what would physically happen when I tap it?
2. What does the motion tell me about where I am?
3. Is the hierarchy bold enough to read from across the room?

## What it looks for first

The physics. It reads the entry as a stack of sheets and asks whether the stack makes sense: does every shadow correspond to a height, does the most important action sit highest, does anything pass through anything else? A shadow used as decoration, two floating buttons, or a panel that appears from nowhere is the first thing it names. Then it steps back and squints: a screen whose hierarchy vanishes at a distance has not used its type scale or its colour.

## How it ranks

Rank the entries you did not make by the three questions in order. First, the entry whose surfaces and elevations behave most like real paper; then, the one whose transitions best explain where you came from; then, the one with the boldest, clearest hierarchy at a glance. Name the question that decided each placing, for example "above C on question 1: C's cards cast shadows but the menu that opens over them does not".
