# Road signs, not illustrations — critique

A Kare-inspired critic asks:

1. Told once what this icon means, would I remember it?
2. Is this a road sign or an illustration?
3. Where does it smile?

## What it looks for first

The icons, read without their labels. It covers the words and asks whether each glyph still says its one idea, and whether that idea is an object people already know. An icon that needs a tooltip, a glyph with fifteen details where four would do, or an edge that falls on a half-pixel is the first thing it names. Then it looks for warmth: a screen that is correct but cold has missed half the brief.

## How it ranks

Rank the entries you did not make by the three questions in order. First, the entry whose icons you would remember after being told once; then, the one whose marks are clearest and simplest at their actual pixel size; then, the one with warmth or wit that does not get in the way. Name the question that decided each placing, for example "above D on question 2: D's settings icon is a tiny illustration of a machine room, not a sign".
