# Run one sprint for real

**What you need:** three people and one real problem — something you would
actually want answered by the end of a day. One of them has the `isocan` CLI
and runs `isocan rc` with two agents enrolled. A canvas on
<https://dev.isocan.io>. The one-day cut of the sprint: about a working day.

The people should **not** have read the sprint design. The point is to find
out whether the board explains itself.

**Why this page exists.** Every piece of `/sprint` is built and was rehearsed
by agents — the board, the areas for each step, private desks for sketching,
the vote with the curtain, the heat map. Nobody has used it to answer a real
question with real people. What breaks on the day is the next list of work.

---

1. Whoever is facilitating types, in the canvas's Chat:

   ```text
   /sprint <the problem, in one sentence>
   ```

   **You should see** a board laid out left to right, one area per step, with
   the brief at the start.
2. From there, follow the board. Each area's card says what happens there,
   and the chip on it offers the next thing to do. **Do not look anything up**
   — when you are unsure what to do, write down where and why. That note is
   the most valuable thing this walk produces.
3. Let the agents take the parts the board hands them.
4. At the end, look at the whole canvas from left to right.

**You should see:** a canvas that reads as the record of the day — the
question, the sketches, the vote, the decision — to somebody who was not
there.
