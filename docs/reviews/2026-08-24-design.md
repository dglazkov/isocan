# Design review — 2026-08-24

Second run. The priority was the new `DesignSystemView` — a document *about*
design craft, so its own craft is the argument.

**Verdict.** The idea is right and the execution is one measurement short of it.
Every token really is drawn as the thing it describes, references really do
resolve through the same `resolveToken` the CLI uses, and the numbers that get
compared down a column really are `tabular-nums`. But the view's single
*claim* — that a palette which cannot carry text says so here rather than in an
audit three weeks later — is arithmetically incapable of being made.
`readableInk` measures each swatch against black-or-white, whichever wins, and
the worst colour in the entire sRGB cube still wins at **4.58:1**. There is no
colour it can fail. Meanwhile the design system sitting on `prj_CIhLRcdYrD`
right now declares `text-muted: #858688` on `surface: #f1f3f4` — **3.27:1**,
against prose in the same file that says `text-muted` is "for dates, counts and
placeholders". The viewer prints **5.8** beside it and says nothing. Core
already has `checkDesign`, which is a real checker; `isocan design check` runs
it; the view does not import it.

**The single change that would help most:** measure the swatch against the
document's own ground, not against black-or-white. `contrastRatio(value,
tokens.colors.surface ?? .background ?? .neutral ?? .canvas)`, marked when it is
under 4.5 — and render `checkDesign(doc)` into the `.ds-problems` block that
already exists and currently shows parse errors only. That is the difference
between a page that prints numbers and a page that finds things.

**Method.** Headless Chrome over CDP against the built app the daemon serves,
on a scratch canvas (since deleted) carrying two real DESIGN.md files rendered
at the item size `isocan add` actually gives them, in light, dark, and `system`
with the OS emulated both ways. Contrast walked up the ancestor chain,
compositing translucent backgrounds *and* every ancestor's `opacity`, until an
opaque ground was found. Every rendered number below was independently
reproduced from the tokens by arithmetic, and the two agreed to 0.01. Probe
sources are in the session scratchpad, not the repo.

## Findings, worst first

### 1. The contrast badge cannot report a failure, and the palette on the canvas has one

`designview.ts:15-22` picks black or white, whichever wins, and prints that
ratio. Searched the sRGB cube at a 3-step: **the minimum of
`max(onWhite, onBlack)` over every colour is 4.580:1, at `#008196`.** So the
badge's floor is above the 4.5 line it exists to police. Rendered on the two
real design systems on hand:

| document | colours | lowest ratio the badge printed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stripi (20 colours) | 20 | **4.58** (`primary-soft #665efd`) |
| Stitch (20 colours) | 20 | **5.14** (`accent #6056f0`) |

Forty swatches, forty passes. The header comment claims this is one of "two
things this can do that a picture of a design system cannot"; a picture would
have scored identically.

What it missed, computed from the same tokens with the repo's own
`contrastRatio`:

```
Stitch:  text-muted (#858688) on surface        (#f1f3f4) = 3.27:1
         text-muted (#858688) on surface-raised (#e5e7ea) = 2.94:1
         text-muted (#858688) on surface-sunken (#dbdde1) = 2.68:1
Stripi:  ink-mute   (#64748d) on canvas-cream   (#f5e9d4) = 3.95:1
         ink-mute-2 (#61718a) on canvas-cream   (#f5e9d4) = 4.13:1
```

`checkDesign` misses them too — it only knows the token *names* `primary`,
`neutral`, `background`, `surface`, and pairs exactly one ink with one ground —
so `isocan design check` says "nothing to fix" for both files.

**Fix, as a value.** Ground the ratio: pick `tokens.colors.surface ??
.background ?? .neutral ?? .canvas` as the ground, print
`contrastRatio(value, ground)` for every colour, and mark the ones under 4.5.
Keep the black-or-white figure as the swatch's *ink* choice — that part is
correct and is what makes the number legible on the chip. Then import
`checkDesign` and render its findings into `.ds-problems`, which already exists
(`DesignSystemView.tsx:53-62`) and today carries parse errors only. Both halves
are already written in core; the view imports `parseDesign`, `parseHex` and
`resolveToken` from the same module and stops one export short.

### 2. At the size the app gives the item, the design system is entirely below the fold

Rendered, at the 420×320 that `isocan add` assigns a new document:

```
.ds-view    clientHeight  318      scrollHeight 15,359     = 48.3 screens
.ds-masthead offsetHeight 280      = 88% of the visible view
first .ds-chip (swatch)   top 509  = 191px below the fold
section tops: Colours 314 · Typography 1,097 · Spacing 3,080 · Radii 3,297 · Components 3,514
```

So the reader of a design system gets: a name, a version pill, and a paragraph
of prose. Not one token. The masthead's description (`:1847`) is
`max-width: 62ch` with no line limit, and the two real documents' descriptions
are 249 and 490 characters — the field the format uses for a *summary* is the
field that eats the card. The screenshot bears it out: at 150% zoom the Stitch
item shows its name, its version, six lines of description, and exactly one
clipped row of three swatches out of twenty.

`⇧F` ("grow an item to what is in it") does not rescue it either:
`packages/web/src/lib/measure.ts:5` caps at `MAX_FIT = 2400`, which is **16%**
of 15,359.

**Fix, as a value.** Three declarations and one default:
`.ds-masthead p { display: -webkit-box; -webkit-line-clamp: 3; -webkit-box-orient: vertical; overflow: hidden; }` —
three lines at 13px/1.5 is 59px, which puts the first swatch row at ~288px and
inside the fold. And give the role its own arrival size the way images already
get one: a `role=design-system` item should land at 560×720 (what the human-placed
one on `prj_CIhLRcdYrD` was hand-resized to) rather than 420×320.

### 3. Three of the view's own labels are under 4.5:1 in every theme, all by `opacity`

`opacity` is a colour decision taken where no colour test is looking.
`tokens.test.ts` cannot see it; `accent.test.ts` cannot see it; a diff reads it
as taste. `--ink-soft` on `--card` is 5.15:1 in light and 5.12:1 in dark, which
means the entire dimming budget is **0.936 / 0.913** — below that the text is
no longer legible text.

Measured on the running app, identical in light, dark, `system`+OS-dark and
`system`+OS-light:

| rule | line | opacity | light | dark | needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `.ds-section > h3 span` (the "20" beside COLOURS) | `:1854` | 0.7 | **2.85** | **3.21** | 4.5 |
| `.ds-ref` (`{colors.primary}` beside the resolved value) | `:1901` | 0.75 | **3.12** | **3.48** | 4.5 |
| `.ds-ratio` (the contrast number itself) | `:1865` | 0.85 | **3.77** | **3.77** | 4.5 |
| `.cursor-chip em` (the live cursor's status line) | `:1607` | 0.85 | 3.50–4.87 | 3.50–4.87 | 4.5 |

`.ds-ratio` is the sharpest of these: it prints "4.6" at 3.77:1. The badge that
reports a contrast ratio is under the ratio it reports, and by Finding 1 it can
never have more than 0.08 of headroom to spend.

**Fix, as a value.** Delete all four `opacity` declarations and reach for a
token instead. `.ds-section > h3 span` and `.ds-ref` already sit in a
`--ink-soft` context and are already smaller than their neighbours — the size
is the hierarchy, and 5.15:1 is quiet enough. `.ds-ratio` should be flat
`#fff`/`#000` as `readableInk` chose. `.cursor-chip em` wants `font-weight: 400`
against the chip's 600, which it already has, and no dimming at all.

**This one has a test.** `packages/web/test/dimmed.test.ts`, added by this run
and green (4 cases, 99ms). It derives the opacity budget from the tokens
themselves, so it expires if `--ink-soft` is ever brightened enough to make
dimming safe; it proves `readableInk`'s 4.58 floor by search, so it expires if
that function is fixed per Finding 1; and it ratchets the four rules the way
`accent.test.ts` ratchets its fourteen. Mutated seven ways before being
believed — a new dimmed-text rule, an entry fixed, `--ink-soft` brightened,
`readableInk` re-grounded, an `opacity` on a non-text rule, an `opacity` inside
`@keyframes`, and a faded `:disabled` control — the four that should fire fired
the right case, and the three that should stay silent stayed silent.

### 4. `.ds-prose` renders the same markdown as `.md-view` with two rules where `.md-view` has twelve

Both mount `<ReactMarkdown remarkPlugins={[remarkGfm]}>`. `.md-view`
(`styles.css:586-597`) styles `h1/h2/h3`, `p/ul/ol`, `pre` (with
`overflow-x: auto`), `code`, `img` (with `max-width: 100%`), `table`, `th/td`,
`hr` and `blockquote`. `.ds-prose` (`:1906-1907`) styles itself and `h3`.

Measured by putting **the same file on the same canvas twice**, once with
`--prop role=design-system` and once without:

| | `.md-view` | `.ds-prose` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `table` | `border-collapse: collapse`, 12px | `separate`, 13px |
| `th` | `background: rgb(240,241,236)`, `1px solid` | transparent, `0px none` |
| `td` | `1px solid` | `0px none` |
| `code` | `background: rgb(240,241,236)`, 11.96px | transparent, 13px |
| root scroll | fits | `scrollWidth 415` vs `clientWidth 382` |

That last row is the visible one: a GFM table in the prose overruns the view by
**33px**, which puts the whole `.ds-view` into horizontal scroll
(`scrollWidth 433` against `clientWidth 418`). The design system's prose renders
*worse* than an ordinary `.md` file sitting beside it.

**Fix, as a value.** One selector. Change `:587-597` to read
`.md-view h1, .ds-prose h1, …` — or, cleaner, add `.ds-prose` beside `.md-view`
in each of those eleven rules, since the two want identical markdown internals
and only differ in their heading scale, which `:1907` already overrides.

### 5. Fifteen thousand pixels of content that no keyboard can reach

Measured on the rendered view:

```
.ds-view   tabIndex -1   focusable descendants 0   scrollHeight 15,359 / clientHeight 318
.md-view   tabIndex -1   focusable descendants 0   scrollHeight 10,987 / clientHeight 318
```

`CanvasViewport.tsx:79` handles the wheel over a scrollable item, so a mouse
works. A keyboard has nothing to focus and nothing to press: `Tab` never lands
inside, so `↑`/`↓`/`PageDown` never apply. SC 2.1.1.

This is the app's pattern rather than the new view's invention — `.md-view` has
the same shape — but `.ds-view` is the extreme case, at 48 screens against
`.md-view`'s 35 for the identical file.

**Fix, as a value.** `tabIndex={0}` on the scroll container plus a name, on both
views: `<div className="ds-view" tabIndex={0} role="region" aria-label={name ??
"Design system"}>`. Two attributes, and the existing focus ring already draws.
(`role="region"` on a scroller is what makes the tab stop justified rather than
noise.)

### 6. The block that draws a spacing scale is written off no scale

`.ds-view` through `.ds-prose h3` is 72 lines. Counted with comments stripped:

- **14 distinct spacing values in 37 declarations** — 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10,
  11, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20. **25 of 37 (68%) are off the 4px grid**, above the
  file's own 64% average.
- **4 distinct radius literals** — 2, 3, 7, 8 — and **zero uses of
  `var(--radius)`**, which is `8px`. `.ds-component` (`:1892`) and
  `.ds-problems` (`:1904`) both write `border-radius: 8px`, restating the token
  rather than using it.
- **7 distinct `font-size` values in 72 lines** — 10, 10.5, 11, 11.5, 12, 13,
  17 — three of them half-pixel, over the six that `slop.ts` ("Type with no
  scale") calls the ceiling for one page.

`slop.ts` names the shape directly: *"margins and paddings in unrelated values
(13px, 22px, 7px) rather than steps of a unit."* A block whose *subject matter*
is a spacing scale and a radius scale is where that lands hardest.

**Fix, as a value.** Nothing new is needed to start — `var(--radius)` already
exists and is already `8px`, so `:1892` and `:1904` are a two-character edit
each. The rest waits on the scale that Finding 6 of the last review asked for
and that nobody has chosen; I am still not writing a guard for a scale that
would be my taste rather than the repo's.

### 7. Content the document states is silently dropped

Two things a DESIGN.md can say that the view never renders:

- **Prose before the first `##`.** `parseDesign` only accumulates lines after a
  `##` heading (`designmd.ts:210-221`), and the view renders `doc.sections`, not
  `doc.body`. Measured on the Stitch document: the H1 `# Stitch` is 8 characters
  that exist in `body` and in no section, so nothing renders it.
- **`omitted:`.** The Stitch document declares `omitted: ["elevation",
  "motion"]` — the author saying, on purpose, what this system does not cover.
  `checkDesign` honours it (`designcheck.ts:28-34`); the view has no branch for
  it at all.

**Fix, as a value.** Render `doc.body.split(/^##\s/m)[0]` above the first
section when it is non-empty, and add one line to the masthead:
`{omitted.length > 0 && <p className="ds-omitted">Not covered: {names.join(", ")}</p>}`.
"What this system deliberately does not say" is one of the more useful lines a
design system has, and it is currently the only declared field the view throws
away.

### 8. `lengthPx`'s comment promises an honest failure; the bar draws 2px instead

`designview.ts:24-30` says a value that is not a drawable length "is not a
length we can draw to scale, and **says so**". `Scale` (`DesignSystemView.tsx:158-181`)
computes `width: ${((n ?? 0) / widest) * 100}%`, and `.ds-bar` (`:1880`) carries
`min-width: 2px`. So `clamp(8px, 2vw, 24px)` — legal in the format, and exactly
the case the comment was written for — renders as a 2px bar, indistinguishable
from a very small step and shorter than every real one. Nothing says so.

Two neighbours of the same shape: `lengthPx`'s regex accepts a leading `-`, so a
negative step yields a negative percentage, which CSS discards, which leaves the
grid's `1fr` — a negative spacing value draws the **longest** bar on the chart.
And when *every* step is unparseable, `widest` falls back to `1` and all bars
render at the 2px floor together, which reads as a scale rather than as an
absence.

This is the same failure mode as standing finding 5 — a comment that describes
behaviour nobody wrote — in a new file. Worth noting as a pattern; it is not yet
a third occurrence.

**Fix, as a value.** `{n === null ? <span className="ds-bar-unknown">not a
length</span> : <span className="ds-bar" style={…} />}`, and clamp the number to
`Math.max(0, n)`. The `<code>` beside it already prints the raw value, so the
reader loses nothing.

## What is good — do not break these

- **The premise, and the part of it that works.** Every token really is drawn as
  itself. Verified on the rendered page: the type specimens carry the declared
  face (`sohne-var, "SF Pro Display", …`) at the declared size (56 / 48 / 32 /
  26px), the spacing bars are proportional to the widest step (2/4/8/12/16/24/32/64px
  → 9/19/38/55/74/111/147/295px), and the components' `{colors.primary}`
  references resolve — 15 components on the Stripi document, each showing the
  resolved hex, a swatch dot, *and* the original reference. Keeping both is the
  right call and is not the obvious one.
- **`readableInk` returns `null` rather than a guess.** "A value we cannot read
  is a value we cannot grade, and inventing a number for it would be worse than
  printing nothing." That instinct is correct and correctly implemented; Finding
  1 is about the ground it measures against, not about its honesty.
- **`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, three times** — on `.ds-swatch code`,
  `.ds-ratio` and `.ds-step code`. Exactly the three places numbers are compared
  down a column, and nowhere else. That is a person looking at the page.
- **Every section returns `null` when it is empty.** Five `if (… .length === 0)
  return null` guards, so a partial DESIGN.md produces no empty headings and no
  "Colours 0".
- **The accent ratchet held across a whole new component.** `accent.test.ts`
  passes both cases: the known fourteen still exist and nothing was added.
  `.ds-bar` reaches for `--accent` as a `background`, which is what the token is
  for. The guard the last run wrote did the job the last run wrote it for.
- **The layer scale held too.** `grep -c z-index` over the 72-line `.ds-*` block
  → **0**. New floating UI did not invent a number.
- **The composer growth is careful work.** Measured: 29px at rest → 365px full,
  capped at `max-height: 40vh` (365.2px in a 913px window) with the reason in
  the comment, `overflow-y: auto` past the cap, the highlight backdrop still
  pixel-aligned with the textarea at full stretch, and `height: auto` written
  first so the field shrinks back after sending. The comment at
  `MentionField.tsx:138-144` records the StrictMode render loop that the naive
  version causes. `grow` is used at exactly one call site
  (`MainThreadPanel.tsx:328`), which is full-height, so the `vh`-based cap is
  safe today.
- **The suite is green and honest.** `npm test`: 105 files passed, 1032 tests
  passed, 55 skipped — all of them the emulator-gated cloud suites.

## What I could not judge without a designer

- **Whether the masthead description belongs in the view at all.** Clamping it
  to three lines (Finding 2) is the small fix, but the format's `description` is
  a paragraph written for an agent's prompt, not a page header. Putting it in a
  collapsed row, or behind the name entirely, may be the better answer — that is
  a call about who the view is for.
- **Whether a swatch should show one ratio or several.** Finding 1 proposes
  measuring against the document's ground. Real systems have three or four
  grounds (Stitch has `surface`, `surface-raised`, `surface-sunken`), and
  `text-muted` fails on all three at different numbers. One number per swatch is
  legible and incomplete; a small matrix is complete and is a lot of ink in a
  104px cell.
- **Whether `.ds-chip` needs a checkerboard.** A swatch whose value is
  `#ffffff` renders as white on `--card`, which is white, distinguished only by
  the 1px `--line` hairline; in dark, `ink: #0d253d` is nearly the card. Both
  documents on hand have such a colour. A transparency checkerboard is the
  conventional answer and would also handle `rgba()` values, but it is visual
  noise on a page that is otherwise very quiet.
- **Whether the half-pixel type sizes are a decision or an accretion.** Still
  open from the last run, and the new block added three more (10.5, 11.5 and a
  17px masthead that matches nothing else in the file).
