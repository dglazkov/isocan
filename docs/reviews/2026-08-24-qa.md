## 1. Placement and fit: the search was tested, the geometry was not

`packages/core/src/placement.ts` shipped over the weekend and now runs **inside
the reducer**, so where a dropped file lands is part of replay. That raises the
price of every hole in it.

**15 mutations on `placement.ts`, 7 killed as found.** The eight survivors were
not edge cases:

| Survived | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `overlaps()`'s entire y test → `a.y === b.y` | **Two boxes only count as vertically on top of each other when their tops are identical.** A box one pixel lower is "clear". Whole core suite green, 340/340. |
| `PLACEMENT_CLEARANCE = 12 → 0` | The clearance is the difference between "not overlapping by arithmetic" and "not a pile". Nothing saw it go. |
| `stepX = want.width + GAP → want.width` | The lattice loses its gap; displaced items no longer line up with each other. |
| tie-break `rank(a) - rank(b)` reversed | The documented "right before down before left before up" was never checked. |
| the nearest-cell distance sort flattened | Same. |
| `MAX_RINGS 14 → 0` | The fallback path fires always, and nothing noticed. |
| the fallback's `y: want.y → 0` | Nothing reaches the fallback at all. |

The y-axis one is the finding. It survived because **every existing case
collides along x** — the tie-break sends a displaced item right, so a canvas of
same-sized items lays itself out as a row and the vertical half of a symmetric
predicate was never exercised. The permissive x mutation (`a.x === b.x`) was
killed immediately; its mirror image was invisible.

**Added to `packages/core/test/placement.test.ts`** (11 new cases): a
`describe("overlap is symmetric")` that collides a neighbour above, below,
left and right of the asked-for spot with neither coordinate shared; a
clearance case; and a `describe("where a displaced item goes")` that asserts the
exact cell, the lattice pitch across a nine-item batch, and — by filling all
29×29 cells the search will look at — the give-up path. Nine new cases.
**13 mutations re-run against them, 13 killed**, including
`PLACEMENT_CLEARANCE → 1`.

One of those tests was wrong first time and is worth recording, because it is
the exact failure this persona exists to catch. `treats a gap narrower than the
clearance as occupied` originally computed its fixture as
`size + PLACEMENT_CLEARANCE - 6` — so setting the constant to 0 moved the
fixture too, and the test passed for the wrong reason. It now uses a literal 6
and asserts `PLACEMENT_CLEARANCE > 6` separately. **A test that derives its
fixture from the value under test is a test that cannot fail.**

**13 mutations on `fit.ts`, 8 killed as found.** Three real survivors (the
other two are variants of one equivalent mutant, below):

- `if (item.width !== t.width || item.height !== t.height)` → either half alone. Every existing case changes BOTH dimensions, so an item that is the right width and the wrong height would silently not be resized — and `fitMoves` returning nothing is indistinguishable from nothing needing to change. Two new cases, one per axis.
- Sorting by x-then-y instead of y-then-x. The existing "anchors on the first in reading order" uses a corner-to-corner pair where both orders agree. New case uses a pair where they disagree.

Three new cases; **3 mutations re-run, 3 killed.**

**Not a survivor, and worth saying so:** the bug `fit.ts`'s own comment warns
about — "an earlier version skipped the check for the first item, which let it
grow straight over a bystander" — is genuinely caught by
`keeps clear of items that are not being fitted`. I mutated it three ways
before believing it.

## 2. Spatial navigation: the bug was fixed on one arm of a four-arm switch

`⌘Up walked sideways` was fixed and tested today. **14 mutations on
`spatialnav.ts`, 7 killed.**

The headline: **applying the identical one-character regression to Down
(`node.y > current.y`) and to Left
(`node.x + node.width < current.x + current.width`) left the whole web suite
green, 178/178.** Up is guarded by three tests written from the real canvas it
was found on. Down, Left and Right inherited nothing. This is lesson #4 —
a test written around the incident rather than the invariant — with the
incident being *today's*.

Also survived: `ORTHOGONAL_WEIGHT 2.5 → 1` (the docstring's "~2.5× keeps a walk
inside its own lane" was unmeasured); `nearestToPoint` measuring from an item's
top-left corner instead of its middle (every fixture in the file is the same
size, so corner and centre agree); ties resolving to the last candidate rather
than the first; and deleting the `return 0` that makes overlap free, which
makes overlap *pay* — a 3000px-tall item 1900px away scores −2100 against an
aligned neighbour's honest 100.

**Added to `packages/web/test/spatialnav.test.ts`** (20 new cases): the
one-pixel-nudge case stated once and run over all four directions, each with a
negative control so a rule that answers null to everything cannot pass; a
bracket on `ORTHOGONAL_WEIGHT` (one case needs w > 2, one needs w ≤ 3 — room to
tune, none to delete); the overlap-is-a-floor case; tie determinism; and
centre-versus-corner. Thirteen new cases. **12 mutations re-run against them,
10 killed** — the two that live are `ORTHOGONAL_WEIGHT = 3`, which is inside
the bracket on purpose, and the equivalent self-skip below.

Two of those tests did not work first time — my weight bracket was out by an
order of magnitude (100 + 10w vs 1000 needs w > 90, not w > 9) and my
straddling item was not tall enough to go negative. Both were caught by
re-running the mutation rather than by reading the test, which is the only
reason to do it in that order.

**Equivalent mutant, named so the next run does not chase it:** deleting
`if (node.id === current.id) continue`. Self can never satisfy a strict
clearance test for a rect with real width and height, and every caller passes
one.

## 4. The silent-measurement shape, elsewhere

That lesson was filed as a second `#5` and is renumbered **#8** here, since the
list already had one; the design run's new lesson had landed as a second `#6`
and is now `#14`, and both are joined back onto the single table they had each
fallen off. Two duplicate numbers in one weekend is a sign the file is being
appended to faster than it is being read. It reads *a measurement that reports zero when it is
broken*. The
generalised form in a test suite is: **a check that iterates a set derived from
a file, and whose answer for "the parser found nothing" is identical to its
answer for "everything is fine."**

I broke the parser in every file-derived test in the repo. Two were vacuous:

- **`packages/web/test/oneblock.test.ts`** — `declares each class once, unscoped` counts duplicates in `bareClassSelectors(css)`. Make the selector regex match nothing and there are no duplicates. Green. This is lesson #1's guard.
- **`packages/web/test/layers.test.ts`** — `uses the scale for anything that can meet another component` filters `declarations()`. Make the `z-index:` regex match nothing and there is nothing outside the scale. Green. A z-index written across two lines would do it for real.

Both fixed with one assertion each on the size of the derived set.
**Proved both ways:** with the new line the broken parser fails the file;
with the line stripped and the parser still broken, it passes.

Correctly guarded already, and worth copying:

- `oneblock.test.ts`'s own `state classes` case (`expect(modifiers.size, "no chained modifiers found — the parser is wrong").toBeGreaterThan(10)`) — the file contained the right pattern next to the wrong one.
- `accent.test.ts` pairs its ratchet with `keeps the known list honest`, so an empty parse makes all 14 known entries look stale and fails.
- `marketing.test.ts` and `changelog.test.ts` both assert their match count before looping.
- `surface.test.ts`'s `registers the commands this test knows how to read`.

`ISOCAN_REQUIRE_EMULATOR=1`. **I did not add it** — it is a CI change, and the
graders are the design persona's territory. Flagging it as the one-line follow-up.

## 6. A standing finding closed: the @-menu offered a name that reaches nobody

`lib/mentions.ts:50` used `session.label ?? session.actor.name` — the **stamped**
name, frozen when the session started. So renaming yourself to "Di" mid-session
left the @-menu offering "Dion 2", and picking that entry wrote a mention that
resolved to nobody.

`actorsAnswerTo` does not save this: it walks the actors the *canvas*
remembers, and a live session on a canvas that person has not written on yet is
not one of them — which is the case in the first test in `mentions.test.ts`.

**Fixed:** `mentionRoster` calls `sessionName`, and pushes the registry name as
a mention candidate so the name the menu just offered actually resolves. Three
new tests. **4 mutations, 4 killed:** the original stamped-name form, the
registry wrongly beating an explicit `--label`, the name being offered but not
mentionable, and a blank label winning.

This is the first of the seven to be closed, and it was closed because it was
the one that did not need a browser. `packages/cli/src/main.ts:3872` is the
next one that does not.

## What is good, specifically

- **`--selftest` is the best instrument in the repo.** It is the only thing here that tests a measurement rather than a behaviour, and it survived every attempt to make it lie.
- **The four `fit.ts` invariants that mattered are genuinely guarded** — bystanders avoided, siblings settled against each other, sizes applied before positions, and the documented first-item bug. Seven of eleven mutations died on the first run.
- **`spatialnav.test.ts`'s existing Up cases are written from the real canvas, with the real numbers.** That is the right way to write a regression test; the only fault is that it was written for one direction.
- **`accent.test.ts`'s paired ratchet** (§4) is the pattern every list-of-known-exceptions test in this repo should copy: the list and the parse check each other, so neither can rot alone.
- **The skip design still holds at 55.** Ten more cases joined the emulator-gated suites during phases 9 and 10, every one of them skipped with the reason in the describe title, and `ISOCAN_REQUIRE_EMULATOR=1` still turns the skip into a failure.

## Gaps not filled, and why

- **Still nothing drives a browser.** Six `session.label ??` call sites remain and none can be verified from here. The suite is honest about this — it does not claim browser behaviour it has not driven — but the class of bug in §6 is live in six more places.
- **The port hypothesis is still unproven and three copies of `freePort` remain** (§3). I would rather leave a named, measured hole than ship a third change justified by a theory I have twice failed to confirm.
- **`--selftest` is not wired into CI** (§5) — deliberately left as a one-line follow-up for whoever owns the workflow.
- **The descendant-tail half of lesson #1** is unchanged from last run.
- **The 55 cloud tests still could not be executed** — no `gcloud`, no JRE on this machine.
