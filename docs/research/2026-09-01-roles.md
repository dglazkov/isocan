# Roles: a ladder, not a matrix

**1 September 2026.** Research. Nothing built.

The request: a permissions model like Google Drive's — **Owner, Editor, Canvas
Viewer, Presentation Viewer** — where each role can do everything the ones to
its right can.

That "each can do the ones to the right" is the important half of the request,
and it is worth naming before anything else: it makes this a **ladder**, a
total order, and not a matrix of per-action toggles. A ladder is one
comparison — *is what you hold at least what this needs* — and a matrix is a
policy engine. Drive itself is a ladder with two checkboxes bolted on, and the
checkboxes are the part nobody can predict.

## What exists today

Less than it looks, and differently arranged than you would guess.

```ts
export type Capability = "edit" | "view";
```

Grants carry it, the door copies it onto the admission it writes, and every
write surface refuses a `view` admission server-side — `/api/ops` by hand,
everything canvas-scoped by one `onRequest` hook. The app never decides;
`CanvasPage` reads `capability` off the socket's hello and, if it is `view`:

```tsx
if (capability === "view") {
  return <Viewer canvasId={canvasId} itemId={itemId ?? null} />;
}
```

**`Viewer` is the deck.** The current slide full screen, arrows to flip, no
canvas underneath and no way into editing chrome. So the rung that exists
today is not a read-only canvas at all:

| Requested | Today |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Derived from `project.createdBy`. Not a grant, not transferable. |
| **Editor** | `capability` absent, or `"edit"`. |
| **Canvas Viewer** | **Does not exist.** |
| **Presentation Viewer** | `capability: "view"`. |

The model is one rung short in the middle, not two. That is a much smaller
piece of work than the request sounds like, and it is the reason to write this
down before building anything.

## The naming falls out for free

The obvious move — `view` becomes the canvas rung and presentation gets a new
word — silently changes what every existing grant means. Somebody shared a
deck yesterday with `--link view`; on deploy those people would land on the
whole canvas instead.

So take the other assignment:

| Rung | Wire value | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner | `own` | Everything, including who else holds what |
| Editor | absent / `edit` | Today's editor, unchanged |
| Canvas Viewer | `read` | The canvas, read-only — new |
| Presentation Viewer | `view` | Today's `view`, unchanged |

**Nobody's access changes on deploy**, because the only value in the wild
keeps meaning exactly what it means now, and the new rung gets a word nothing
has ever written. That is worth more than a tidier vocabulary.

## Where the ladder lives

One place, in core, and everything else asks it:

```ts
const RUNGS = ["view", "read", "edit", "own"] as const;   // ascending
export function atLeast(held: Capability, needed: Capability): boolean;
```

Then:

* the `onRequest` hook asks `atLeast(held, "edit")` where it now asks `!==
  "view"`;
* the grants route asks `atLeast(held, "own")` where it now calls
  `ownsThisCanvas`;
* `CanvasPage` picks a surface by rung rather than by one equality;
* the CLI's `--link` takes any of the four.

The refusal stays server-side. The app may hide a control it knows will be
refused — that is courtesy — but a rule the app enforces and the daemon does
not is a habit, which is the one law this project actually has.

## The Owner decision, which is the real question

Everything above is mechanical. This is not.

Today an owner is **derived**: `project.createdBy`, checked against the badge's
claims. It cost nothing to add, it cannot be tampered with, every canvas that
predates it already has one — and it is why the reported lockout is now
impossible, because the person who made a canvas can always change what its
link allows.

It is also unmovable. Nobody can be given ownership; nobody can hand it over
on the way out of a company; a canvas whose creator has left is permanently
governed by an actor nobody can be.

Two ways, and I would take the second:

**Ownership stays derived, and `own` is grantable on top.** `createdBy` is the
*floor* — always an owner, never removable, so a canvas cannot be locked out
of itself — and additional owners are ordinary grants at the `own` rung. That
is Drive's model minus its sharpest edge, which is the dialog that lets the
last owner remove themselves.

**Ownership transfers.** Closer to Drive, and it needs a rule the floor gives
for free: what stops the last owner leaving? Drive answers with "you must
name a successor". That is a fine answer and it is a second flow, a second
refusal, and a second thing to get wrong.

The floor costs nothing and removes a whole class of "I locked myself out".
Transfer can be added later *on top of* it; it cannot be sensibly removed
later from underneath.

## Decisions this forces, which are worth making on purpose

**Can an Editor invite?** Today, yes — any editor may create a grant, and only
the *capability* of the link is owner-only (that asymmetry is deliberate:
inviting is additive, changing the link sweeps everybody). Drive makes this a
checkbox. A ladder says sharing belongs to `own`. I would move invitation to
`own` and keep the answer honest — "ask <name>, who made this" — because the
alternative is that Editor is really Owner with extra steps.

**Is there a Commenter?** Drive has one between Viewer and Editor. It was not
asked for, and comments here are not annotations — they are the channel that
reaches agents, so a "commenter" can start work. That makes it a much bigger
rung than it looks. Leave it out; note that it will be asked for.

**What can a Canvas Viewer see?** Everything a reader can: items, versions,
comments, the history. Presence is the question — should a viewer appear in
the facepile? I think yes, and visibly as a viewer: a person reading over your
shoulder is a fact about the room, and hiding it is worse than showing it.

## What it would take

* `core` — the rung order, `atLeast`, and the parse for `--link`. One fold.
* `server` — three call sites change shape rather than logic: the hook,
  `/api/ops`, the grants route. Plus `admit` copying the rung onto the
  admission, which it already does for two values.
* `web` — `CanvasPage` chooses among three surfaces instead of two; the Share
  dialog's two buttons become four; the new read-only canvas is the existing
  canvas with writes hidden, which is the largest single piece here.
* `cli` — `isocan share --link read`, and `isocan share` naming the rung per
  row.

No new op and no migration: a grant without a capability still means edit, and
`view` still means the deck.

## Recommendation

Build it as a ladder with the four rungs above, `read` as the new word, the
creator as an unremovable floor, and `own` grantable on top of that floor.
Move invitation to `own` in the same change, since a ladder that stops short
of who-may-share is a ladder with a hole in it.
