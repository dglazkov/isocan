# Sheepdog

**4 September 2026.** A design, written before anything is built. As asked:
*a user can create a sheepdog, a "pet" that you own, and choose which
canvases it hangs out on (default: all you have access to). You can tell it
to listen only to you, to certain people, or to everyone, and turn that on
and off quickly — if you are paying for tokens, for example. It needs to be
cloud-homed and auto-woken.*

The short version. **A sheepdog is a standing agent with an owner.** Almost
all of it exists: one name on many canvases, a wake that carries its canvas,
a ceiling per agent, a facepile that says *standing by* rather than *here*,
and `isocan history <actor>` that says where it stands. What a sheepdog adds
is three facts the standing-agents project never needed, because a standing
agent belonged to a machine and a sheepdog belongs to a **person**: *whose*
it is, *whom it listens to*, and *whether it is on*. Those three facts want
to live in one place, changed by one gesture — a **kennel record** at the
home. Cloud-homing it is the one genuinely new mechanism, and it is the one
the on-demand design sketched as *isocannery* and deliberately left: a
hosted `isocan rc` that sleeps when nobody is talking to it and is woken by
a **signal** from the home — never a command. Four ways to do that are
weighed below, and the recommendation is to build the three cheap facts
first, on the owner's own machine, so the cloud half is a deployment of a
working thing rather than a design of an imagined one.

This is the emissary from the Tiny Stitch thesis, in its smallest true
form: an agent of *yours* that shows up where you are not, spends what you
allow, and answers whom you say.

## What already exists, read from the code and the designs

- **One name, many canvases.** The enrolment key is the name, not
  `(canvas, name)`, so one badge holds one actor across canvases
  ([standing-agents, mechanism 1](../projects/standing-agents/design.md)).
  `isocan rc --all` parks one process on every canvas the machine's
  enrolments name, with the ceiling (twelve turns an hour), the cycle guard
  and the session handle **per agent across them**, not per canvas — the
  budget rule a sheepdog on forty canvases must keep.
- **The wake.** An rc parks; an op arrives; `dispatchReason` in
  `packages/core/src/inbox.ts` decides whether it is for this agent:
  *mentioned*, *main-thread* and *in-your-thread* pierce any filter, your
  own ops never wake you, and `AgentRules { items, ops }` narrow the
  changes. The summons carries `ISOCAN_CANVAS`, so the agent's shells act
  on the canvas it was called to.
- **The rule that governs all of it**, from the standing-agents research:
  *what runs is configured locally and only locally; what arrives from the
  canvas is the signal, never the command.* The canvas holds an **offer**
  (`agent.enroll`); the machine holds the **wiring** (`rc-agents.json`).
  The home never grows a spawner — on-demand chose the rc over a daemon
  that spawns for exactly this reason, and it is the reason "cloud-homed"
  cannot mean "the home runs it".
- **Custody.** The web offers *Add an agent* only while it can see a live
  rc; enrolment is a handshake the rc completes, so the actor is born on
  the machine that answers for it and the desk needs no new rule
  ([agent-custody](../projects/agent-custody/design.md)).
- **Presence that tells the truth.** *Answerable* is connection-bound; the
  facepile shows *standing by* in words as well as a ring; a roster never
  fills with the absent.
- **The piece deliberately left.** A second machine answering as the same
  actor still needs a vouch. The
  [one-agent-many-canvases](2026-08-29-one-agent-many-canvases.md) note's
  shape A — **an actor credential the agent keeps**, revocable before it
  exists — is what lets an agent arrive somewhere else without a person
  present. A sheepdog in a box is exactly that agent.
- **The owner floor.** `project.createdBy` is an owner nobody can remove
  ([roles](2026-09-01-roles.md)); ownership is derived, and grants sit on
  top. A sheepdog's owner is the same kind of fact.
- **isocannery, sketched** in the
  [on-demand design](../projects/on-demand/design.md): a service that hands
  you a `wss://` ACP endpoint with your agent and your credentials behind
  it and sleeps it on `end_turn`. Its requirements — sleep and wake, hold
  credentials so isocan does not, bound creation, accept a release message
  — are this feature's requirements, written a week early.

## The three facts a sheepdog adds

**1. Whose it is.** An actor with an `owner`: a person's id, set at
creation, unmovable in the roles-note sense (the owner is the floor; a
sheepdog is never an owner of anything). Everything else derives from it:
where it may go, whose word it obeys, whose bill it runs up. An agent
today is owned by the machine that runs it — `rc-agents.json` on a laptop.
A sheepdog is owned by a person wherever it runs, which is what lets it
run somewhere that is not the person's laptop.

**2. Whom it listens to.** A gate on the *speaker*, which the routing
composition does not have. Today a mention pierces every filter — right for
a team's agent, wrong for a pet you pay for, where the whole point of
*listens to me* is that a stranger's `@Rex` costs you nothing. So it is
an outer gate applied before `dispatchReason`, not a fourth rule inside it:

| Setting | What wakes it |
| --- | --- |
| `owner` (default) | arrivals whose actor is the owner |
| named people | arrivals from the owner or anyone on the list |
| everyone | the composition as it stands today |

Ops from actors outside the gate never reach the composition, so they
never count against the ceiling either. What the person outside the gate
sees has to be honest: their mention does nothing, and the facepile should
say why — *Rex · listens to Dion* — the way *standing by* already says the
agent is not here. A silent mention is the one thing this must not do.

**3. Whether it is on.** One switch. Off means: the rc releases every hold
(so the facepile stops saying *standing by* the instant the switch flips —
liveness stays connection-bound, no TTL), the box sleeps, and no arrival is
read until it is on again. It is not withdrawal: the offers stay on the
canvases, the actor keeps its name and its history, and on is one gesture
back. It is also not the ceiling: the ceiling stops a runaway; the switch
stops spend on purpose. The two should be visible together — *on · 7 turns
today · listens to you* — because a switch you cannot read the cost of is
one you leave on.

**Where the three live: a kennel record at the home.** One record per
sheepdog, keyed by actor: `{ owner, listen: "owner" | ids[] | "*", on:
boolean, canvases: "all" | { except: ids[] } | ids[], wake?: address }`.
Not on the enrolments, because the switch would then be N ops on N
canvases and a flip mid-write would leave a pet half asleep; not on the
machine file, because the owner flips it from a phone and the box must
read it, not the laptop. It is home state, which is why the rule survives:
*listen* and *on* are **narrowings** — the canvas can only ever make the
sheepdog do less — and the wiring (harness, credentials, directory) stays
where the box is. Nothing in the record can name a command.

## Which canvases: derived admission, not N enrolments

"All I have access to" is a moving set — a person is admitted to a new
canvas tomorrow, loses one next week — so a list copied at creation is
wrong by Friday. Two ways:

- **Enrol it everywhere, now.** An `agent.enroll` on every canvas the owner
  can reach, and again for each new one. Forty ops, forty offers on forty
  rosters, and a nightly to catch up. Honest on every canvas; expensive and
  stale between runs.
- **Derive it: a sheepdog is admitted wherever its owner is, one rung
  below.** The desk answers "may this actor read canvas X" by asking "may
  its owner", capped below `own`. Admission follows the owner in both
  directions with no op, and the exclusion list in the kennel record is
  the only thing a person edits. The roster shows the sheepdog only where
  it is *on* and *standing by* — presence is still connection-bound, so a
  derived admission does not put six faces on a canvas nobody works on.

The second, on the roles note's own reasoning: a derived fact cannot be
tampered with and cannot go stale, and a grant can be added on top later.
The offer still lands per canvas the **first time the sheepdog acts
there** — `agent.enroll` written by the rc as the turn starts — so the
record on any canvas is indistinguishable from CLI enrolment, the design
goal custody already states.

## Cloud-homed and auto-woken: four ways

The rc is the same program on a laptop, a VPS, or a rented box — on-demand
built it so. The question is only *what holds the box's socket open, and
what wakes it when nothing does*.

**A. The home runs it.** The Cloud Run instance spawns the harness when
an arrival matches. Refused three times already: the home never grows a
spawner (remote code execution by design), the home is one instance with
a thousand-socket ceiling and no room for forty harnesses, and the tokens
would be spent on isocan's key, not the owner's. Named to be crossed out.

**B. A hosted rc that sleeps — isocannery, built.** A box per sheepdog
(e2b pauses with memory intact and resumes in about a second; a Fly
machine or a Cloud Run job does the same more slowly) running `isocan rc
--all` with the owner's harness credentials held **in the box**, vault
style, so isocan never sees them. Awake, it holds sockets exactly as a
laptop rc does and is answerable. Asleep, it holds nothing — and here is
the hinge: **a sleeping box cannot be summoned over a socket it does not
have.** So the home needs one small new outward mechanism: the kennel
record's `wake` address, which the home calls when an arrival passes the
gate for an actor whose rc is not answerable. The call carries the canvas,
the seq and the reason — the same three the standing-agents research said
a wake must carry — and nothing else. The box wakes, the rc reconnects,
reads what happened with `isocan context`, and acts. A signal to an
address the owner registered, never a command from a canvas: the rule
holds. Cost at published e2b rates: a small box awake twenty minutes a day
is about a dollar a month; left running, about fifty.

**C. A scheduled runner.** No box holds anything; a cron (the shape the
graders and the night shift already use, `grade.yml` at 08:23 daily) runs
`isocan rc --all --once` every few minutes as the sheepdog, reads its
inbox, acts, exits. Nothing new at the home. The price is honesty: a
sheepdog that answers in five minutes is never *standing by*, and the
facepile must not pretend it is. Cheap, ugly, and a fine fallback tier for
a box that has been destroyed rather than paused.

**D. The owner's own machine.** `isocan rc --all` today, plus the kennel
record. Not cloud-homed: it sleeps when the lid closes. But it is every
mechanism except the box, which makes it the right first version — the
gate, the switch, the words, the derived admission, all walked against a
real day before a box is rented.

**What every cloud way needs first: the actor credential.** A box is a
second machine. Today a second machine answering as Rex needs a person's
vouch, and there is nobody at 3am to give one. Shape A from the
many-canvases note — a long-lived secret the actor holds, presented as the
vouch, **with revocation built before it** — is the prerequisite, and it
is the piece standing-agents left on purpose. The kennel record is where
the revocation lives: destroying the record revokes the credential, and
the box's next connect is refused with a reason.

## The gestures

- **Make one.** `isocan sheepdog new Rex` — claims the actor with
  `owner = you`, writes the kennel record with the defaults (listens to
  you, on, all canvases), and prints the two things a box needs: the
  actor's credential and the release word. In the web: a *Get a sheepdog*
  door, which unlike *Add an agent* does not need an rc in sight, because
  the box is what will answer — custody's set-aside invitation flow,
  arriving with its reason.
- **Point it.** `isocan sheepdog listen --to dion,maya` /
  `--to everyone` / `--to me`; `isocan sheepdog on|off`;
  `isocan sheepdog canvases --except <ref>`. One row in `isocan who` and a
  chip on the face for each: *listens to you · on*.
- **Read it.** `isocan history rex` already leads with where it stands, a
  row per canvas. Add the cost line: turns today, turns this week, what
  the ceiling stopped.
- **The switch, quickly.** ⌘K *Rex: off* / *Rex: on*, and the same on the
  face's peek. Off must land in one round trip and show in the facepile
  before the dialog closes — the hold is dropped by the rc on reading the
  record, so the face changes when the socket does, not on a timer.

## What would make this fail

- **A silent gate.** A person mentions a sheepdog that does not listen to
  them and nothing says so. The mention must be answered by the *facepile*
  — *listens to Dion* — if not by the dog.
- **The wake becoming a command.** The `wake` call must carry canvas, seq
  and reason and nothing more; the box reads the rest itself. The moment a
  payload can say *what to do*, anyone who can post on a shared canvas can
  choose what runs in somebody's box.
- **A credential with no revocation.** Built in the wrong order, a leaked
  credential is an identity forever. The kennel record's deletion is the
  revocation, and it exists in stage 1, before any box.
- **Per-canvas budgets.** A sheepdog on forty canvases with a ceiling per
  canvas is forty budgets nobody approved. The ceiling stays per agent, as
  standing-agents already ruled.
- **Derived admission drifting above the owner.** A sheepdog admitted one
  rung below its owner can never be granted higher; if grants on top are
  ever allowed, the cap is the owner's own rung.
- **isocan learning which box.** The rc must not branch on whether it runs
  in e2b, on Fly, or on a laptop. If it does, on-demand's warning applies:
  two products with one confusing bill.

## Recommendation, in stages

1. **A sheepdog on your own machine.** The kennel record at the home
   (owner, listen, on, canvases), the speaker gate before the composition,
   derived admission one rung below the owner, `isocan sheepdog new | listen
   | on | off | canvases`, the words in the facepile and in `who`, and the
   cost line in `history`. `isocan rc --all` reads the record as its rules.
   Proof: Rex on every canvas Dion can reach, answering Dion and nobody
   else, switched off from a phone and gone from the facepile before the
   thumb lifts.
2. **The actor credential, with revocation first.** Shape A: minted at
   `sheepdog new`, presented by any rc as the vouch, revoked by deleting the
   record. Proof: the same Rex answering from a second laptop with no person
   asked; a revoked Rex refused with the reason.
3. **The kennel: a hosted rc that sleeps, and the wake signal.** One box
   provider (e2b, for the pause), credentials held in the box, `wake` in
   the record, the home's outward call carrying canvas, seq and reason.
   Proof: the laptop closed, a mention from Dion at 3am, Rex awake in
   under five seconds and asleep again after the turn; the bill readable.
4. **The scheduled tier and the shop window.** Cron as the fallback for a
   destroyed box, honest in the facepile; *Get a sheepdog* in the web with
   the cost beside the switch.

Stage 1 is worth doing whether or not the rest happens: it is the
standing-agents walk finished for a person rather than a machine, and
every later stage is a deployment of it.
