# An inbox for the person

**29 August 2026.** Research. Nothing built.

The question: *if anybody on any canvas @-mentions me, I should be able to see
it in one place and answer it — and be told, whichever canvas I happen to be
looking at.*

**Short answer: the rule already exists, is already tested, and already runs
across canvases — for agents. `isocan wait` is this feature. The person has no
equivalent.** What is missing is not the definition of "for me"; it is a place
to stand, a channel that is not per-canvas, and one decision this product has
so far deliberately deferred.

---

## What exists, read from the code

**A mention is resolved and stored, not re-parsed.** `Comment.mentions` is
*"actor ids @-mentioned in the body, resolved at authoring time against the
actors the author could see"*. So finding every comment that mentions somebody
is a scan over threads with an equality test — not a re-run of the matcher
against today's names, which would quietly change what an old comment meant.

**"For me" is already written down, and it is more than a mention.**
`isocan wait` decides with `addressesMe` plus two more rules, and the reasoning
is in the source: a comment is for you when it mentions you (by actor id, or by
a name you answer to including your session label), **or** it lands in the main
thread — the designated channel, always actionable — **or** it lands in a
thread you are part of, because you wrote in it or were mentioned in it.
Everything else is *ether: not actionable*.

That is the inbox's filter, finished, in code that has been running for weeks.
Any second definition written for the person would be a second answer to a
question that has one.

**`wait` already polls several canvases.** The cross-canvas half is not new
either.

**Unread already has a shape.** `unreadByAuthor` counts comments newer than a
per-thread seen-mark, and the rail and the facepile both read it.

---

## The three real problems, in the order they bite

### 1. Read state is local, and that is a position rather than an oversight

Seen-marks live in `localStorage`, keyed `isocan.seen.<canvasId>.<actorId>`.
The front door says so as a design commitment: read state is on the
**ephemeral plane** — *"what you have seen, kept by you"* — beside presence and
viewport, and explicitly not on the durable one.

An inbox makes that position expensive for the first time:

- An inbox that says **"3 unread"** is saying *"3 unread in this browser"*. Open
  the same account on a laptop and it says something different, and both are
  "right".
- Worse, it can only compute unread for canvases **this browser has already
  opened**, because a canvas never visited has no seen-mark. A brand-new
  mention on a canvas you have never opened is exactly the case the feature is
  for, and it is the case the local record cannot see.

So the feature forces a decision that has been deferrable until now: **does
"what I have read" become a fact the canvas keeps?**

Three answers, and the middle one is probably right:

- **Leave it local.** The inbox becomes "mentions, newest first" with no read
  state at all — a list you scan, not a count you clear. Honest, cheap, and
  weaker than what was asked for.
- **A read marker per actor per thread, durable.** An operation like every
  other fact, replicated and undoable. It is the isomorphic answer and it costs
  the thing the ephemeral plane was drawn to protect: **every glance becomes a
  write.** A person opening ten threads writes ten ops that everybody's replica
  receives. The plane exists precisely so that looking is free.
- **A high-water mark per actor per canvas.** One durable value —
  *"I have seen everything up to seq N"* — instead of one per thread. A glance
  writes at most one op per canvas per session, it answers "is there anything
  new for me here" exactly, and it does not try to model which individual
  messages were read. **This is the one to design first**: it buys the whole
  inbox and keeps the cost sub-linear in attention.

### 2. The home is the right place and currently the wrong one

The home page is a good instinct — the ask is right that this should not be
"the number of canvases you have" — but it is narrowed on purpose. Its comment
is explicit: it lists **the canvases this origin is the home of**, not every
canvas the daemon holds, because every title is a client-side `<Link>` and a
wide list would be *"a working button that opens a local, stale second copy of
a canvas that lives at dev.isocan.io"*.

An inbox inherits that narrowing and it is wrong for an inbox: **a mention from
a teammate is most likely to be on a canvas that lives at their home, not
yours.** So the inbox needs what the list deliberately refused — canvases from
several homes — and it needs to open each one **at its own address** rather
than locally. That is not a contradiction; it is the difference between a list
of things you can open here and a list of things that want you. But it has to
be built as the second thing, not as a widened version of the first.

### 3. "Whichever canvas I am in" needs a channel that is not per-canvas

The socket is per canvas — that is what makes replay-from-seq work. A
notification that arrives while you are looking at a different canvas has no
socket to arrive on. Three options, in ascending cost:

- **Poll from the home page only.** Cheapest, and it fails the actual request:
  you are told when you are already looking at the inbox.
- **Poll from anywhere**, on a slow timer, from whatever page is open. Blunt
  but honest, and the daemon can answer it in one read.
- **A home-level socket** carrying nothing but "something addressed you, on
  canvas X, at seq N". Right in the long run, and it is a second live channel
  with its own reconnect and replay story — the thing to do once the first
  version has proved somebody wants it.

## What I would build, and in what order

1. **`isocan inbox`** — the CLI first, and not for tidiness: it forces the
   derivation into core where the app must read the same one, and it is
   testable without a browser. **BUILT 29 Aug**: `inboxOn` in
   `packages/core/src/inbox.ts`, `isocan inbox [--mentions] [-n]`. The rule
   moved out of `wait` rather than being restated, and a guard fails if either
   surface starts deciding a reason of its own.

   Two things the build settled that the design had not. **Your own words are
   never in your inbox** — obvious once said, and exactly what this filter
   forgets, because you are in every thread you wrote in. And **one canvas
   failing to answer must not silence the rest**: an inbox that empties because
   a replica is unreachable lies in the only direction that matters.

   First reading on this machine: **7 named you · 153 in the Chat · 8 in
   threads you are in.** That ratio is itself a finding — the Chat is twenty
   times the volume of being named, which is the argument for `--mentions`
   existing on day one and for any future count being per-reason rather than
   one number.
2. **The high-water mark**, as an operation, once the list has proved useful.
   That is what turns a list into an inbox and a count into a truth.
3. **The home's panel**, reading the same derivation, opening each canvas at
   its own address.
4. **Notification**, cheapest first: a poll on a slow timer. A home-level
   socket when somebody is annoyed by the latency, and not before.

## What would make this fail

- **A second definition of "for me".** If the app grows its own filter, the
  inbox and `isocan wait` will disagree, and the disagreement will be invisible
  until somebody is not told something.
- **A count that is a lie on the second device.** The most likely shape of
  failure, and the reason §1 is first. A list with no count is better than a
  count that is wrong somewhere else.
- **Every glance becoming a write.** The ephemeral plane is drawn where it is
  so that looking is free. A per-thread read op would make attention itself
  replicate.
- **An inbox that fills with your own agents.** Most `@mentions` on a busy
  canvas are people addressing agents. The filter is *addressed to ME*, and
  "me" must not quietly include the agents wearing a persona on your behalf —
  which is a question that gets sharper the moment
  [one agent works on many canvases](2026-08-29-one-agent-many-canvases.md).
