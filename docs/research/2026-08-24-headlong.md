# What Headlong knows

**24 August 2026**

The question: *what can isocan learn from
[Headlong](https://github.com/laude-institute/headlong) — an open-source agent
microharness built around persistent agency, with trajectory and context as
first-class tools?*

The short answer: **one idea to take almost verbatim, one gap it found in this
repository today, one hole it confirms from a fourth direction, and one
principle to decline on purpose.**

**On provenance.** Read from the repository's `README.md`, `AGENTS.md`,
`philosophy.md` and `docs/shellm.md`. The `bin/traj` and `bin/context` sources
were not read, so the tiering parameters below are described as the project
describes them and no formula is claimed. Every isocan fact was measured in
this repository today.

---

## What it is

A complete agent harness in **under 10K lines of Bash** — "a handful of small
Bash executables, each doing one thing well and composing through pipes, files,
and environment variables." Ken Thompson's philosophy, aimed at agents.

The tool surface, verbatim: `shellm` (the engine), `llm`, **`traj`**
(trajectory operations), **`context`** (renders trajectory into LLM messages),
`thinkers`, `chat`, `focus`, `mem`, `skills`, `recap`, `persona`, `identity`.

Three mechanics matter here:

**The trajectory is an append-only JSONL DAG.** Runs live under
`~/.headlong/trajectories/`; the first line is a `{"type":"trajectory"}` header
and the next a `shellm-run` step carrying command, workdir, model and
environment. Sub-runs **fork as branches inside the parent's trajectory file**
rather than nesting as child directories, and the DAG has merge as well as
fork.

**Context is a render of that trajectory, at decaying resolution.** "The entire
trajectory stays in context at exponentially decaying resolution. Recent
entries appear verbatim, and older entries are progressively summarized. The
tiers act as an index." And the principle underneath it: **"Nothing is
compacted away in place. Compaction and agent introspection operate on the same
files with the same tools."**

**The agent thinks when nobody is talking to it.** This is the thesis:

> "A message from a human does not start a session. It lands in the agent's
> thought stream as one more observation, and the agent decides if and when to
> respond."

with a pacing rule attached — "the rate of thinking backs off exponentially
when nobody is talking to the agent and resets the moment a message arrives" —
and a price: **$1 to $2 an hour** at their settings.

## 1. The idea to take: backoff, not cron

isocan's agent model is the exact opposite of persistent agency, and says so.
`isocan wait` blocks until a message arrives that is *for you*, and the guide
is emphatic: **"Your turn ends inside `wait`, or it ends wrong."** Between
messages an isocan agent is not thinking slowly. It is asleep.

That is the right default for a shared canvas — an agent that acts unbidden on
someone else's board is a problem, not a feature — and it should stay the
default. But it leaves [the night shift](2026-08-24-the-night-shift.md) with no
primitive at all, because a night shift is precisely *"the agent keeps working
when nobody is talking to it."*

The obvious build for that is a cron job, and Headlong's pacing rule is
strictly better:

| | Cron | Exponential backoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fires | at 02:00, whether or not there is anything to do | when the agent decides |
| Idle cost | one full wake-up per tick | decays toward nothing |
| Someone speaks | waits for the next tick | **resets instantly** |
| Feels like | a scheduled job | a colleague who is around |

The night-shift research named "cost, unbounded and unwatched" as a failure
mode and had no answer for it. This is the answer, and it comes with an
arithmetic: at Headlong's stated $1–$2 an hour, an eight-hour night is roughly
**$8–$16 per agent** — a number to design a budget against rather than
discover.

The synthesis worth building is not "replace `wait` with a thought loop". It is
**`wait` with a floor**: park as now, but wake on a decaying timer as well as
on a summons, and let the agent decide whether there is anything worth doing.
`wait --timeout` already returns exit 2 on a quiet timeout and the guide
already says a timeout is "the quiet half of a conversation, not the end of
one" — so the loop shape exists. What is missing is the backoff and the
mandate.

## 2. The gap it found here today

Headlong's principle — *nothing compacted away in place; compaction and
introspection use the same files and the same tools* — sent me to look at
isocan's own compaction. isocan passes the first half and fails the second, and
the failure is one line wide.

`isocan gc` compacts the oplog to an undo horizon (`--keep-ops`, default 500).
Dropped entries are **not deleted** — `compactOplog` appends them to
`oplogArchiveFile` before rewriting the live log. The history is kept, exactly
as the principle asks.

Then, measured:

```
who WRITES the archive?   file-store.ts:347   (one appendFile)
who READS it?             — nothing —
```

`isocan tail` reads the live oplog only. **No verb on either surface can see an
archived operation.** isocan preserves its history into a file that nothing in
the product can open.

That is this repo's own recurring shape wearing new clothes: lesson #13 is a
guard nobody runs, #14 is a check that cannot say no, and this is **a record
nobody can read**. It is also cheap to fix — `tail --archived`, or a `--since`
that falls through to the archive — and worth fixing before the night shift
starts generating history faster than a person can watch it.

## 3. The hole it confirms, from a fourth direction

Headlong's trajectory is a DAG **with fork and merge**. isocan's oplog is
strictly linear: `{seq, envelope, inverse}`, one sequence per canvas. isocan
does have forking, but it lives in the *canvas* — items carrying `parent=` —
rather than in the log. And it has no merge at all.

That is `docs/projects/atlas/convergence.md`'s hole, and this is now the **fourth**
independent arrival at it:

1. The [market survey](2026-08-23-agents-on-the-canvas.md) — every canvas tool
   shipped divergence without convergence.
2. The atlas journey's Scene 8, which cannot be finished without it.
3. The [night shift](2026-08-24-the-night-shift.md) measurement — 9 fan-outs
   against 2 deliberate choices.
4. Headlong, arriving from a data model rather than from a product: a
   trajectory that forks and never merges is not a DAG, it is a tree that
   leaks.

Four lines of work reaching one gap is most of an argument for building it.

## 4. The principle to decline, on purpose

Headlong's Thompson test is real and isocan fails part of it deliberately:

> "Every piece of state is a text file on disk… small composable tools, text as
> the universal interface, the filesystem as the state layer."

isocan's state *is* text on disk — `oplog.jsonl`, blobs, `actors.json`,
`.isocan/project.json`, and a design system that is a DESIGN.md rather than a
dotfile. But it is not reachable *as files*. A **single writer** owns it: one
daemon, one queue, one reducer, twenty-eight operations.

That is not an oversight, and the trade should be stated rather than
apologised for. The single writer is what buys the properties this product is
built on — two surfaces that cannot diverge, undo that is per-actor and exact,
a log that replays, and a canvas several agents can write to at once without a
lock. Composable tools over shared files buy inspectability and cost you the
guarantee; one vocabulary through one writer buys the guarantee and costs you
composability at the file layer.

Where isocan *does* pass the Thompson test is the part that matters most for
agents, and it is worth noticing because Headlong's critique names it exactly.
"The function-calling approach to LLM tooling has an enumeration problem" —
and isocan does not hand an agent a tool schema. It hands it a **CLI and a
guide** (`isocan --agent-help`), and the agent writes shell. Same instinct,
arrived at independently.

The [voice research](2026-08-24-voice.md) is where the enumeration problem
bites this product for the first time, because the Live API *does* want
function declarations — which is another reason its synchronous constraint is
worth respecting rather than working around.

## 5. Two smaller things worth stealing

**`recap` as a verb.** Headlong makes summarization a tool, and its context
tiering makes the summary an *index* into the full history rather than a
replacement for it. An isocan agent joining a canvas today reads
`comment list` and `activity` and forms its own impression, every time, from
scratch. `isocan recap` — the canvas's history at decaying resolution, recent
verbatim and older summarized, with ids that fly you to the thing — would be
the same idea on a surface that already has both the history and the ids.

**Why a subagent exists.** Headlong: "A subagent can see why it was created,
what the parent already tried, and how it fits into the big picture." isocan
tracks lineage carefully for *artifacts* — `parent=` is what makes the canvas a
tree instead of a pile — and not at all for *agents*. An agent spawned by
another arrives knowing only the canvas. The thread it was pointed at carries
some of the why; nothing carries what has already been tried.

## What this survey did not establish

- The tiering formula. `bin/context` was not read; the exponential-decay
  description is the project's own, and no parameters are claimed here.
- Whether the DAG's *merge* is semantic (results folded back) or structural
  (branches rejoined in the file). The distinction matters for what isocan
  could copy, and the README does not settle it.
- Anything measured about Headlong running. This is a reading, not a trial.
  The $1–$2/hour is their figure, not a reproduction.

---

## The one-line answer

Take the **backoff** — it is the pacing model the night shift needs and the
answer to its unbounded-cost risk, with a price tag attached. Fix the
**unreadable archive**, which this survey found by taking Headlong's
compaction principle seriously for ten minutes. Build **convergence**, which
four independent lines of work have now asked for. And keep the single writer,
because the guarantee it buys is the product.
