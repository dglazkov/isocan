# Teleport: moving a canvas to another home

**1 September 2026.** Research, and the first half built the same day — the
move, as recommended. Two departures from what is written below are marked
**[built differently]** where they occur; the second half, two writable homes,
remains deliberately unbuilt.

The question, in two parts: *take a canvas and send it to a different home —
and then keep an existing copy up to date, replaying the ops that arrive.*

Asked as an alternative to [#91](https://github.com/dglazkov/isocan/issues/91),
which proposes deleting the local daemon. It is a better question, because it
attacks the same pain — a canvas is stuck at the home it was born at — without
removing the thing that makes offline work and an agent's hands on real files
possible.

The two halves have very different answers, and separating them is most of
the value here.

## What a canvas actually is

Four things, and only the first is the canvas:

* `oplog.jsonl` — every operation, in order, each with a `seq`.
* `canvas.json` — the fold of that log. Derived; a cache.
* `blobs/` and `blobs.json` — the bytes items name, content-addressed.
* `project.json`, `trash.json` — the record and what was deleted.

**The log is the canvas.** The snapshot is what you get by replaying it, and
the reducer is deterministic, so a home holding the same log holds the same
canvas. That is the whole reason teleport is tractable: it is not a data
migration, it is a replay.

Two things do *not* live in that directory, and both matter:

* **The actor registry** — the current name, colour and face mark for every
  actor — is at the home and, in the engine's own words, "home-scoped and
  never replicates down".
* **The desk** — badges, grants, who may enter — is at the home too.

## The half that is buildable

**Move = replay the log at the new home, carry the blobs, and leave a
signpost.**

Every piece already exists in some form:

* **The blobs** are content-addressed and there is already a mechanism for
  "make two homes agree about bytes": `reconcileBlobs`, which now runs on a
  timer. Teleport is a reconcile with a different destination.
* **The forwarding** exists. A canvas whose home is elsewhere already renders
  *"This canvas lives at https://isocan.io — open it at its home"*, and
  `homes.json` is the per-machine routing table that answers it. A teleported
  canvas leaves exactly that behind.
* **Adoption by marker** half exists. `.isocan/project.json` is committable
  and resolves by walking up like `.git`, and a marker naming a canvas this
  machine has never seen already materializes it "under the same id".

**Keep the seq numbers.** The temptation is to replay through the ordinary
write path and let the new home assign 1..N. Do not: replicas and parked
agents hold seq cursors, and renumbering silently invalidates every one of
them. Write the entries verbatim — the log is the log — and a cursor that
survives the move is a `tail()` that does not notice it happened.

**Carry the actor rows for actors the log mentions.** *[built differently: the
move does not carry them, and says so instead — see below.]* Otherwise everyone
arrives stripped: current names revert to whatever was stamped on each op
months ago, colours fall back to the ones ids imply, and every face mark
vanishes. This is the failure the registry was built to prevent — "Dion 2
still talking in a thread after Dion 2 became Di" — reintroduced by the move.
It is a small, boring copy, and forgetting it would be the most visible thing
about a teleport.

*What shipped names this rather than doing it.* The registry is home-scoped by
construction — the same property that stops a replica leaking names upward —
and a teleport that wrote rows into another home's registry would be the first
thing that ever did. So the command reports it, on the dry run and after the
move both: names, colours and marks stay behind, and people arrive under
whatever was stamped on their ops. That is the failure this section predicts,
made visible instead of prevented. Worth revisiting; not worth punching a hole
in the registry's one invariant to get.

**Do not carry the grants silently.** Who may enter is a decision about a
place, and the new home is a different place with a different operator. A
teleport that quietly recreates "anyone with the link can enter" has widened
access without anybody saying so. Recreate the invitations, show them, and
make the owner confirm the link.

**Stop the old home from accepting writes**, at the moment of the move rather
than eventually. A tombstone that forwards, not a copy that still works: two
writable copies of one canvas is the second half of this document, and it is
not a thing to arrive at by accident.

*[built differently: this is a `homes.bind` rather than a new tombstone.* The
routing table already answers "this canvas lives elsewhere" and the app already
renders the signpost, so binding the old home to the new one gets the forward
for free and leaves the old bytes in place — recoverable if the move was a
mistake, and swept later rather than deleted mid-operation.*]*

## The half that is a different product

*"Keep it up to date and replay those ops"* sounds like more of the same. It
is not, and the reason is one line in the engine:

```ts
const seq = runtime.lastSeq + 1;
```

**Exactly one place assigns order, and that is what makes replay safe.** The
reducer is deterministic given an order; it is not commutative. `item.move`
resolves by who went last. `item.addVersion` stacks in the order it arrives.
Two homes both accepting writes produce two orders, and there is no rule in
the vocabulary for merging them — the ops carry an idempotency key, which
answers "have I seen this one", and say nothing about "which of these two
happened first".

So a second *writable* home is multi-master replication. The honest options
are a designated leader — which is what a home already is — or CRDT semantics
the op vocabulary does not have and would not gain cheaply.

**But one-way following is already built.** A replica follows a home: it
holds the canvas, serves reads fast, and forwards writes upstream. "Keep a
copy at another home current" is a replica with a daemon in front of it, and
the interesting version of the request is not a second home at all — it is
*let a home be a replica of another home*, which is a smaller change than
making two homes agree.

That distinction is worth stating plainly, because the two requests sound
identical and one of them is a weekend and the other is a research programme:

| Want | What it is | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move a canvas to another home | Replay its log there, forward from the old one | Buildable now |
| Keep a copy elsewhere current | A follower — one writer, one reader | Mostly built |
| Write to it in both places | Multi-master replication | A different product |

## Recommendation

Build the move. Do it as one command with a dry run, because it is the kind
of operation people want to see before they do it:

```sh
isocan teleport <canvas> --to https://isocan.io    # --dry-run first
```

Order matters, and it is the same order `reconcileBlobs` already implies:
bytes first, then the log, and only then the routing row. Anything that fails
before that last step leaves the canvas exactly where it was, which is what
makes it safe to try.

*Built, with one thing this section did not anticipate: the log goes*
**verbatim**, *through an `adopt` route rather than a loop over `submitOp`.
`PostOpRequest` carries no `ts` — deliberately, so a client cannot lie about
when something happened — so a replay through the ordinary write path arrives
correctly ordered and entirely re-dated. The seqs this document worries about
would have survived by luck. Every timestamp would not.*

Do not build the second writable home. When somebody asks for it, ask which
of the three rows above they actually mean; it is almost always the second.

## What this leaves open

* **A canvas with agents parked on it.** Their cursors survive if the seqs do,
  but their `wait` is against a URL. Teleport should probably wake them.
* **The undo stacks.** They are per-canvas runtime state, not log entries.
  Moving mid-session and losing an undo stack is defensible; losing it
  silently is not.
* **Which direction the marker wins.** A committed `.isocan/project.json` in a
  repo that half the team has cloned still names the old home until they pull.
  The forwarding signpost covers it, but the first person to be forwarded
  should be told why rather than shown a redirect.
