### motion phase 1: `isocan motion render` records a motion to an H.264 MP4 video item

`c638a76`

A render page of the web app (MOTION_RENDER_ROUTE, under the canvas's
address, lazy, not behind the experiment) reads the motion item's blob into
an inert DOMParser document, drives its stage with core's own engine —
never the document's copy, never the sandboxed iframe's pixels — and
encodes each frame with WebCodecs H.264, muxed by mp4-muxer in the page's
lazy chunk. It refuses in words when the browser cannot encode H.264.
`isocan motion render` opens that page in the headless browser `canvas shot`
uses (scripts/motion-render.mjs, through the new CliHost.runPackageScript,
module API 0.2.11, scripts/*.mjs only), uploads the bytes and adds a
video/mp4 item beside the motion with renderedFrom. Caps: 1080/1920 sides,
60 fps, 60 s, refused in words before Chrome starts.

Proof (scripts/motion-proof.mjs, scratch home): 360 frames, h264,
1080x1080, 6 s in 17 s; decoded frames 0/72/359 within 2.2 mean abs of the
page's own pre-encode pictures and each nearest its own; the hidden-frame
path phase 2 will use decodes to the CLI's frames exactly; the video plays
on the canvas. The design's item-only route was wrong (an item names no
canvas) and is corrected. Open: mp4-muxer is deprecated upstream; the
personal-canvas render path is unexercised.

### motion phase 0: the engine, two recipes, and `isocan motion new`

`29bd7a6`

A motion engine in @isocan/core/motion, our own, written to the shapes of
rossark0/rossark-motion-skill (MIT, credited, no code ported): easings, seg,
track, flow, wave, loop, bell, seeded, every value a pure function of t.
`assemble` and `idle` recipes; principle checks that refuse a rotation with
no pivot and warn on lag, idle amplitude and jumps. `isocan motion new
<svg…>` sanitises the sources (parse5: scripts, on*, foreignObject, remote
hrefs and url() out, ids scoped per layer), inlines them at their canvas
positions in one stage, and adds a text/html motion item with
motion/motionSources/motionSeconds/motionLoop. The document carries the
engine as createMotionEngine.toString(), so the toolchain's own output is
the one source; a bare-vm test and the release-bundle CLI test hold it.

Proof (scripts/motion-proof.mjs, headless Chrome, scratch home): the 1.2 s
frame pixel-identical twice and after render(0.3) (0 of 345,600 differ);
both motions play live on the canvas. Startup-read ceiling 5 → 5.1 MB and
the cold-start guide budget 3,500 → 3,600, each with its dated reason.
Behind the `motion` experiment; no web door yet.
