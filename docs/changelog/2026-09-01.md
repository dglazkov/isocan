# 1 September 2026 — One person on two machines, and one agent on two canvases

A long day with three threads: the multi-identity project designed and
built end to end, a scatter of things a person notices (post-its, the
mark from the terminal, teleport), and the evening's triage — a freeze
whose cause was finally found, a roadmap that had been lying, and the
first phase of standing agents.

## Multi-identity: the same person on more than one machine

The identity desk's mechanism 6 — person resumption across browsers —
was built in phase 9 and played on prod, but no surface reached it: the
door refused a taken name in prose while rendering no control that
proved an address. Five phases, one day.

**The door shows who this browser may be, not only who it has been**
(`e3ac443`). `useResumable()` reads the cached attester offer and
re-reads when a proof lands, through a subscriber list rather than the
one-slot shape `onReBadge` uses, because the door is mounted from three
places. Resumable rows render as known-identity rows, one style, deduped
by id — to the person they are the same act.

**The door starts the proof** (`274bfd4`): five states beneath the name
form, keyed on the attester offer and never on message text. On a home
with no attester the markup is byte-for-byte what it was, held by test.

**The refusal renders its remedy** (`0701d12`). `claims.ts` throws
`name-taken` from two places and both messages were written for the
CLI. The browser now keys on the wire code and renders its own sentence
with a control that opens the address field. The admit refusal named a
menu entry that no longer existed — "Work from your terminal…" became
"Bring your own agent…", the one line the project writes outside
`packages/web`.

**The words on the first machine** (`a66402a`), and a lie caught on the
way: D′ said "nobody else here has proved it", which the door cannot
know — the server's resumable list omits actors the asking badge already
claims. The door now says only that the proof lets you pick up nobody
*new*.

**Two actors become one person** (`b9389e0`): `actor.join {from, into}`
joins the registry family beside `setColor` and `setMark` — home-scoped,
not undoable, refused unless the presenting badge claims both. The log is
never rewritten; readers resolve through a `joined` map. Both surfaces:
`isocan identity --join <id>` and "Fold into…" in the identity menu.

**And the one reader phase 5 missed** (`35fddfd`): `ownerOf` returned
`project.createdBy.id` raw, so a canvas made by "Dimitri 2" stayed owned
by an actor who had stopped being anybody. Easy to miss because the two
surfaces fail in opposite directions — `ShareDialog` compares the
survivor and disables the owner controls; `ownsThisCanvas` asks whether
the badge claims the creator, and the fold does not revoke the claim on
`from`. Raised by the roles research, which wants `createdBy` as an
unremovable owner floor and therefore needs it to name somebody real.

## Things a person notices

**A post-it is a text node wearing paper** (`ea5b4d4`, `016e864`,
`2f0a4f3`, `f49d4b9`, `4c0dc89`). Option A from the morning's research,
built the same day: `properties.paper` on a `kind=text` item — no new
op, no new kind, no migration; strip the property and it is an ordinary
markdown item, the same promise `textnode.ts` already made. Both
surfaces at once. Two follow-ups within the hour are the bugs worth
remembering: the swatches painted nothing because `.text-paper` was
declared sixty lines *above* `.text-style-btn` at the same specificity
(order was the whole fix), and the composer did not wear the paper it
was about to commit — it does now, square at `PAPER_SIZE`, the item's
own tokens. A dimmed placeholder was replaced by a quieter token because
`dimmed.test.ts` is right that opacity on text is a contrast decision no
colour test can see.

**The mark is settable from the terminal** (`782bd5f`). A fact one
client can set and the other cannot is what this repo calls a habit;
`isocan identity --mark ⚓` mirrors `--color` down to the clearing word,
gated by core's `isFaceMark`. It also rides on the cursor now.

**Teleport** (`06c6cc6`, `5dfc914`, `02b53eb`). Asked as an alternative
to #91's "delete the local daemon", and a better question: a canvas is
stuck at the home it was born at. Moving is a replay — the log is the
canvas — so `isocan teleport <canvas> --to <home>` sends bytes, then the
log, then the routing row, in that order so anything failing before the
last step leaves the canvas where it was. Two honest departures from the
research: the actor rows do not travel (the registry is home-scoped by
construction, and the command *reports* the loss on the dry run and
after), and the old home gets a `homes.bind`, not a tombstone.

**Roles as a ladder** (`f6fbc86`): Owner / Editor / Canvas Viewer /
Presentation Viewer, each able to do everything to its right — one
comparison, not a matrix. The surprise is that today's `view` already
*is* the presentation rung, so the missing piece is one rung in the
middle.

**Small honesties:** rename offers itself only when there is a rename
to do (`b77abd0`); a chosen emoji is saved because the store had stopped
listing what it saves (`7666b12`); and an agent starting a fresh
conversation comes back as itself at once instead of being refused for
thirty minutes (`f82dffa`, #89) — a guard meant to protect reincarnation
had been refusing it.

**Three doors for newcomers** (`ea5b4d4`, `695a3e7`, `f639fee`): a
59-line `start.md` that answers "do I need Claude Code?" with no, a
first-hour guide for a third person who does not know which of
`development.md`'s two doors is theirs, and the two lines that connect
an agent, said where a contributor will look.

## The evening: triage, a root cause, and standing agents

**A heartbeating tab can freeze — the cause, then the fix** (#85). The
31 Aug detection put the canvas tip on the heartbeat, read "from the
engine, never counted along the broadcast path." The root cause is a
Cloud Run rollout: `max-instances=1` is per *revision*, a WebSocket is
one in-flight request with an hour's timeout, so the tab's socket stays
on the draining old instance while every new request goes to the new
one. The old instance's engine cache is dropped only when *its own*
append is fenced, and an instance nobody writes through never appends —
so `tipSeq`, which read that cache, agreed with the frozen tab. In this
failure the engine cache *is* the broadcast path. Writes self-heal
through `OplogFencedError`; reads never tripped it. Reproduced with two
daemons over one store: a fresh socket to B is told seq 5, A's beats and
even a fresh hello to A say 2. Two `green` advances bracket the reported
window. The investigation was posted first and the fix followed the same
evening, once asked for: `Store.tipSeq` reads the tip from the backing
(the last oplog line; on Firestore the newest `ops/{seq}` document by
the index it already keeps), the engine drops its cache and announces
`onBehind` when the store has moved past it, `ws.ts` closes the room with
`WS_BEHIND` (4409, the 409 the write fence already wears), and every
client — the tab, the replica's home-link — redials at once rather than
backing off from a failure it did not cause. The hello and the beat carry
`K_REVISION` so the next freeze is attributable without a repro.
`rollout.test.ts` is the shape with two daemons; lessons.md #16 is the
pattern: a cache read as the truth it caches, in the one moment two
processes hold it.

FOUR PHASES DONE". The roadmap is derived from that front matter, so it

**One agent, one name, one machine, many canvases** (standing agents,
phase 1). The wake the research called the hinge was built by on-demand
on 30 Aug, and the rc turned out to be one canvas wide three times over:
the directory's binding, an enrolment key with a canvas in it, and a
summons that handed the agent no canvas. Enrolling Percy on a second
canvas from the same machine was a second session key on the same badge
asking for a worn name — refused by the desk's name rule, #89 in another
coat. The key is the name now (`agent:<name>`), so the same claim on any
canvas resumes the one Percy through `applyClaim`'s ordinary `mine` path;
the summons carries `ISOCAN_CANVAS`, read like `--canvas`, and the `agent
add` containment reads the flags alone so a summons's environment is not
mistaken for a pointer the agent chose. A project directory
(`docs/projects/standing-agents/`) holds the design and the walk; the
multi-canvas rc, `available` in the web roster, and `isocan history` are
phases 2–4.

**A lesson from the build, not the product:** a perl one-liner with
`${agentName}` in single quotes interpolated an empty perl variable, and
`enrolmentKey` returned `agent:` for every name. The tests caught it as
"whoami is Nico" — the machine identity, because no binding matched — and
the probe that found it was reading the badge's rows off `/api/actors`.

## The evening after: a design sprint, as a script over verbs that existed

The [research note](../research/2026-09-01-design-sprint.md) asked what it
would take to run a Knapp/GV design sprint as a multiplayer activity between
people and agents, and answered: almost nothing new. `wait --timeout` is the
facilitator's bell; the phase can be derived from the Chat the way `/ask`
state is; a vote is a reaction; Crazy 8s is `/variation 8`; the supervote
lands as `choose`. Counted across every oplog on the machine: 72 children of
31 parents against 15 convergence ops — divergence habitual, convergence
rare — and reactions never once used as votes.

Built the same evening, stages 0–2 of the note's own staging:

- **`/sprint`**, a built-in slash command — the facilitator's skill. Neutral,
  owns the clock, never votes, sketches or decides; one verb per phase; silence
  enforced by not posting in the Chat during a box (every parked agent wakes
  on it). Not a shipped model: a skill whichever harness the team runs.
- **`core/sprint.ts`** — eighteen phases with Knapp's timeboxes as defaults,
  `parseSprintCommand`, and `sprintState`: the newest `/sprint <phase>` line in
  the Chat, timed from its daemon stamp. No `sprint.setPhase` op; the record is
  the conversation. A hand-in is one property, `sprint=<phase>`. `hidesVotes`
  is the lens half of the curtain; `tally` splits human dots from agent dots,
  an agent being a harness-bearing cli session or an enrolment, never a guess.
- **`isocan sprint [show|phase|end|handin|tally]`**, with `--json` carrying
  `remainingSeconds` so the bell is `isocan wait --timeout $(…)`. `phase`
  refuses a word that is not a phase before posting anything.
- **The clock chip** under the toolbar; reaction chips that show the mark and
  hide the count while a vote is open; the item byline out of the tooltip
  while voting; *Hand in for …* on the item menu while a phase runs; the split
  human/agent count on every chip while a sprint is on. The bell is two
  generated tones and a flash, only when this tab watched the clock reach zero.

Two decisions worth recording. **The curtain is a lens, and the note says so
in words**: the scratch canvas is the real privacy (admission, enforced at the
door), the hidden count is etiquette, and `isocan sprint tally` reads the
record because the facilitator is the referee. And **the app hides votes on
every item, not "wall items"** — a lens that had to know which items were the
wall would be a second definition of the wall. `wallFor` exists for the tally,
where the question is precise. Stage 3 — rival rooms, the overnight Monday —
is practice written into the skill, not code. The sprint has not been run for
real yet, which the note put before everything else.
