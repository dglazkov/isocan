# 24 November 2026 — Slide transitions, smooth: four causes of jank, and three quick content swaps

## Why

"The transitions are a lil janky." And a second ask beside it: subtle,
polished 2D transitions, not 3D, for decks whose slides share a background,
where the content should poof and the next slide's pieces join, rather than
the whole surface moving.

## The jank, measured, and what each cause was

Every transition was screencast at real speed in headless Chrome on a deck
of HTML screens sharing one background. Four separate causes showed up:

1. **The arriving page was not painted yet.** The frame pool warms the next
   slide but hides a frame it has never shown at opacity 0, and a browser
   does not paint what is fully transparent. So a push slid in a WHITE page
   that filled in as it went. A warm neighbour now stands at full opacity
   UNDER the slide on screen, painted and covered, once that slide has
   itself been shown (`data-settled` on the stack).
2. **A page wholly off the stage stops rendering.** Even painted, a push
   that began at `translateX(100%)` and a wipe that began fully clipped
   arrived white: Chrome throttles a cross-origin frame that is entirely out
   of view. Zoom and the content swaps, which start on the stage, never did.
   Every arrival now starts with a sliver on the glass (99.4%, 86°).
3. **The canvas underneath panned during every flip.** `revealItem` ran a
   500ms requestAnimationFrame glide of the hidden canvas, and the selection
   re-rendered it, on the main thread, in the same beat as the transition.
   Full screen now records where to land and snaps the camera (0ms) once the
   flip has settled, or immediately on Esc.
4. **Layers created at the moment of motion.** A frame's first transform
   re-attaches it (measured on the morph, 19 Oct). In a transitioning deck
   every live frame is a layer from the start, the stack's clip is permanent
   rather than switched on per move, and warm neighbours not in the move are
   hidden for its length; a push could otherwise reveal a third slide.

Every keyframe now animates only `transform` and `opacity` (wipe's
`clip-path` is the one exception, guarded by a test). Curves are an expo-out
for arrivals and a short ease-in for departures. The flat motions stay under
half a second, and the morph went from 1.6s to 1.15s, which keeps the
pieces flying but reads as decided rather than staged.

## Three content swaps, and wipe

- **Poof, Drift, Rise** (`pieceSwapPlan` in `core/src/morph.ts`): the
  slide's background holds still and only its pieces move. The old slide's
  pieces poof out about their own centres (or glide aside, or lift off), one
  after another in reading order, and the next slide's arrive the same way.
  Each piece is two keyframes with its own delay, so the compositor plays
  them and nothing is sampled. Chrome in the same place on both slides (a
  footer, a logo) is pinned; a big panel the content sits on counts as
  surface (`isBackdrop`), so a deck whose content sits on one card still
  comes apart piece by piece. Played by the same sandboxed twins as the
  morph (`MorphStage`); for images, text, or before the twins are warm, a
  matching whole-slide stand-in plays instead of a cut.
- **Wipe**: the next slide is revealed across the current one along the way
  you went.
- **Zoom** became subtle: four percent of scale and a cross-fade, not a
  fly-through.

## Guards

`core/test/morph.test.ts` holds the swaps to: no tilt, A's ground still,
backdrops never moving, pinned chrome, two staggered keyframes a piece
inside the length, and poof contracting about each piece's centre.
`web/test/decktransition.test.ts` holds the smoothness rules: layers before
motion, only the two pages in the move, compositor-only keyframes, no
camera pan mid-flip, and no arrival that starts wholly off the stage.
