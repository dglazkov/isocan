# 2 September 2026 — The sprint gets its walls, and a canvas gets a ladder

Three threads. The morning fixed what the Text tool had broken and taught
the wire that "here" means here. The middle of the day gave the sprint a
board: areas as a primitive, the eleven sheets, and a walk across them.
The evening designed the roles project and built its first phase: four
rungs on one ladder, and a canvas a person can read but not change.

## Roles: the ladder, and the read-only canvas

**The project is written** (`75ebcdb`). `docs/projects/roles/` starts from
the 1 Sep research — four rungs, Owner · Editor · Canvas Viewer ·
Presentation Viewer — and adds what one canvas could not ask for: a
**space** (a named set of canvases a grant can name), a **group** (a named
set of people a grant can name), and a **bar** (a row that says no). The
design keeps the comparison in one place (`atLeast`), puts the creator
under everything as a floor with `own` grantable above it, reads both
scopes at the door with the highest rung winning, reads group membership
at the door rather than copying it, and makes the sweep the one recompute,
reporting per badge so a rung change reaches an open socket. An architect
pass against the code found eighteen things the first draft got wrong,
and the docs carry the answers — among them the eleven literal `"view"`
tests that would have stored `read` as edit.

**Phase 1 is built and walked the same evening.** `Capability` widens to
four values; `RUNGS`, `atLeast`, `highest` and `narrowed` land in core,
and `narrowed` is now the one place that decides whether the field is
written — the eleven literal tests are gone. The new rung is spelled
`read` so that nothing already written changes meaning: every `view` row
still opens the deck, and absent still means edit. `admittingGrant` sorts
by rung and takes the creator's actor id, so the floor is applied at the
door and turning the link off no longer expels the creator's own browser.
The re-ask widens to every admission below `edit` and re-roots to whatever
the door now gives. `POST /api/oplog/watch`, which checked no admission at
all, now runs the door test per canvas in its list. The read-only canvas is
the editor with its writes hidden — a list of seventeen entries in
`lib/capability.ts`, each pinned to the file and the gate expression, and
`readonly.test.ts` reads the source and proves each gate is still there.
The Share dialog's link radios become three and the invite field gains a
rung picker; the facepile hover, the roster and `isocan who` say
*reading*. The refusal stays `view-only`, with its message widened.

The proof ran on a scratch daemon borrowing dev's attester, the CLI as the
creator and two Chrome origins as two people. Two things the walk turned
up are recorded in the phase's Findings: a named invitee refused at the
door is offered no way to prove the address (open — the design call is
not the phase's), and the `isocan` binary always carries its daemon's
badge, so the terminal half of a lower-badge proof is a bearer badge
against the same routes.

## The sprint is a board

**Areas** (`ee80be1`, phase 0). A facilitator covers the wall in labelled
sheets, one per phase, and that sheet is an **area**: `kind=area` on an
ordinary markdown item — its title the name, its blob the card, its box
the region — so it moves, undoes, replicates and lists like everything
else, and the wire gains no op type. Membership is derived by geometry,
an item's centre inside the sheet, so nothing goes stale when something
is dragged out. On the web a sheet is drawn behind everything and is
transparent to the pointer except for its title strip and handles. The
CLI gets `isocan area new | ls` and `--in <area>` on `text`, `add`, `mv`,
`ls` and `format`.

**The board and the brief** (phase 1). `SPRINT_BOARD` in core is the wall
as data — eleven sheets, Brief to Wrap — laid by `isocan sprint board` and
the `/sprint` skill in one row, as one undo group, idempotently. The brief
is one card with a history: every `isocan sprint brief` is a new version
of the same text node, never a second card.

**The walk** (phase 2). Calling a phase glides everyone who watched the
call to the phase's sheet, and the clock chip offers the phase's one
action — Go there, New note, Hand in — so nobody needs a phase name, a
tool, or a verb. The first real walk corrected three things: a band
reserved for the sheet's card so a note does not land on it, a tab that
loaded mid-sprint no longer mistakes the load for a call, and the first
call of a sprint walks the tab because the chip now remembers what it saw
on mount. `d1d081f` is the journey and the walk that all of this stands
on: seven scenes, six phases in dependency order, zero new op types as the
target.

## Words land where they were typed

**The composer keeps the words it opened on apart from the words being
typed** (`e80c2cd`). Three Text tool flows broke together, and two were
one bug: `restyle` copied the draft into the baseline, so "type, then
choose a paper" read as unchanged on commit. The baseline is now written
once, on open. Reasoned through as a contract and written into the
post-it doc: a look is not a wording, editing sits on the node, the
select-all waits for the words.

**"Commit this here" means here, and the wire says so** (`923c549`,
`dbd6068`). A note typed touching another was nudged clear on commit. The
first fix exempted text nodes at coordinates; the second found the right
reason — it is the gesture that carries the meaning, not the kind.
`Placement.chosen` puts the intent on the wire: the Text tool's click, a
drop, a paste at a point, `--at` all send it, and `positionIsMeaningful`
leaves a chosen spot alone on the daemon and in the reducer. A
placement guard test pins every producer on both surfaces; a later commit
(`5f6f23e`) makes the guard scan for producers it has not met rather than
naming them.

**The bar says sizes, and previews on hover** (`582b0d1`, `b14a48d`,
`5f6f23e`). Hovering a paper swatch, a step or a face previews it on the
thing being typed into; only a click chooses. The step labels become
S / M / L / XL from one map in core, accepted by `isocan text --style M`
beside `--style heading`, and the buttons grow to a size you can see.
