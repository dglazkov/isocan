### A record reports; it never summons

`f7f83e3`

A voice session posts its transcript to the Chat when it stops, so the
conversation is not lost with the session. The Chat is the canvas's agent↔user
channel and every parked agent wakes on it by design — so each one was handed
a person's spoken half of a conversation with a DIFFERENT agent and read it as
a request addressed to it. The screenshot that filed this shows Scout
beginning to answer a transcript of a person talking to Enceladus.

Routing had no way to tell an ask from a record, because until something
posted a record there was nothing to tell apart. `Comment.record` is that
distinction, and one clause at the top of `reasonFor` is the whole rule — so
`isocan wait`, the inbox and the rc read one definition rather than three. It
is the same sentence the system voice already earns in `dispatchReason`
("reports outcomes; never summons"), one rung lower, so a record posted by a
PERSON gets it too.

**It sits before the mention check**, which is the opposite of every other
rule there and is the deliberate part. Speech is transcribed and transcription
is full of names: "Scout should look at this", said out loud to a voice agent,
is a sentence about Scout and not a summons to Scout. A record that could page
by mention would page somebody on nearly every long session. Addressing an
agent stays a deliberate act — type it, or let the agent you are talking to
comment in its own name.

**Ordinary comment input may carry it, unlike the other typed fields on
`Comment`.** `designDecision` and `design` confer authority, so minting one
has to be impossible; this takes authority away, and the worst an author can
do with it is decline to page people they could simply not have named. The
MODEL still cannot set it: it is absent from the tool declarations, the
planner never sees it, and the panel supplies it out of band through
`runTool`'s new `from` channel — with a test that a model naming it in its
arguments changes nothing.

`turnedAway` also returns false for one: a record asked nobody for anything,
so an rc posting "Scout couldn't answer" under a transcript would be answering
a question that was never put.

The agent guide says what a record is and how to treat it: you will still see
them when you read a thread, and they are the best account of what somebody
was trying to do — context, never instructions.

### Voice: the whole sentence, a wave that fills its row, and the reply's own clock

`a211156`

Four things seen while using it, three of which were one wrong belief each.

**The transcript kept only the last fragment.** `say` replaced the speaker's
line on every piece, on the belief that the provider sends growing partials
("read", "read the", "read the canvas"). It does not. `LiveServerContent` is
documented as an *incremental* update, and the API carries a separate
`interimInputTranscription` for the field that IS re-sent as it grows — two
fields would not exist if they meant one thing. So a whole answer rendered as
`Enceladus: you need on the canvas.` Pieces are appended now, and the reply
writes itself across the panel as it is spoken. A tool row landing mid-turn no
longer splits the sentence around it, and `turnComplete` / `generationComplete`
— rather than a guess at a pause — is what starts the next line.

**The wave sat in the corner of its own row.** The bars were a fixed 3px each
while only their container stretched, so eleven of them drew a 63px stub in a
192px row: "the voice pattern is really small" was literally true. They flex
now — 40 bars dividing the width they are given, measured at 192 of 192.

**The wave and the glow were two reactions to one voice.** The bars took the
raw meter while the beam under them applied its own attack, release and idle
breathing, so they never quite agreed. They are one instrument, so they move on
one number: `VoiceBeam.onLevel` hands back the level it settled on and the wave
draws from that.

**The reply's colour ran ahead of the reply.** The output meter was fed at
`push`, when the PCM ARRIVED. The model sends faster than real time and chunks
are queued against `nextStartTime`, so the meter spiked while the network
delivered a sentence and sat at zero while that sentence was still being
spoken — "it happens quickly", which is what a meter on the wrong clock looks
like. `Playback` now analyses its own output, so the colour follows what is
coming out of the speaker.

**And a burst of tool rows no longer eats the conversation.** Seven
`move_item → done` in a row is one fact repeated; it collapses to one row and a
count, and tool rows are set tighter and dimmer than speech.

Guards for the folding, which is where the wrong belief lived: pieces join, a
tool row does not end a sentence, an end-of-turn does, sealing twice costs one
seal, and a count is a RUN rather than a tally.

### Voice: one wave, coloured by whoever is talking (#337)

`3d67f7c`

Two five-bar meters stood side by side and answered a question nobody
had. The levels are never both high — the two of you take turns — so one
was always flat, and telling them apart meant comparing two columns. One
wave that CHANGES COLOUR answers the question people actually have,
which is whether it is hearing you or answering you, at a glance.

`--accent` is the app's own "this is yours" — it is the send button —
and `--away` is its PRESENCE colour, the other party who is here. So the
pairing is the app's vocabulary rather than two colours picked to look
different. The colour eases over 180ms rather than snapping, and the
flip carries a small margin so a breath while it is answering does not
strobe the colour mid-sentence.

**It reads as a voice rather than a meter** because the bars are not a
bar chart of the level: each takes the level through an arch, tall in
the middle and short at the ends, with a slow per-bar wobble, so a
steady tone still moves and a loud one blooms from the centre. It runs
its own frame loop off the level refs — the level changes many times a
second and React has no business seeing it.

The taps moved with it: the levels are registered once, in an effect,
rather than by whatever happens to be drawing them. The wave and the
glow now read the same numbers, and swapping a component cannot make the
level go quiet — which is exactly how the meters came to be painted onto
nothing.

**And the voice answers by name.** "Voice:" in a transcript is what a
system calls itself; Iapetus is who you picked. Both the transcript and
the block that lands in Chat use it, falling back to "Voice" only when
the provider's default is answering and there is no name to use.

Measured moving: heights form a travelling arch and `data-who` flips
`voice` → `you` with the colour easing between them.

### Voice: five declared tools that could not work, and non-blocking measured (#337)

`8f09986`

"Can you select X" failed, and the reason is worse than a missing tool.
`selection_set`, `selection_clear`, `viewport_focus`, `viewport_pan` and
`find_items` were all DECLARED to the model and unwired in the browser,
and the fallthrough handed back the internal sentinel as the error — the
model was told `{ok:false, error:"__selection_set__"}` and had a token
where a sentence should be. It could not act on that and could not say
why.

Selection and camera are one PERSON's, not the canvas's, so they go
through the host rather than through an operation and nobody else's
screen moves. `reveal` was already there; `select` is its sibling,
handing modules the shell's own selection action so a module's "select
these" and a person's click land in identical state, side effects and
all. `find_items` answers from the facts in the projection's wording.
`viewport_pan` is refused in a sentence that names what DOES work,
because there is no pan-by-delta on the host and inventing one that
scrolled somebody's canvas by a guess is worse than saying so.

**And non-blocking, measured rather than believed.** The docs say
asynchronous function calling is unsupported on Gemini 3.1 Flash Live,
which is what `gemini-3.8-live` reports itself to be. Both halves were
sent:

  gemini-3.8-live        tool call +1152ms, turn complete +1168ms,
                         silence until answered — identical with the
                         field removed. Accepted and ignored.
  2.5-flash-native-audio tool call +1227ms, audio resumed at +3556ms,
                         172ms BEFORE the response landed. It kept
                         speaking.

So the view-only tools now declare `NON_BLOCKING` and their answers
carry `scheduling: WHEN_IDLE` — free on the default model, real on the
other, and the model is switchable in ⌘K for anyone who wants to trade
the newer model for a camera that moves mid-sentence. `WHEN_IDLE` rather
than `INTERRUPT`: the point of not blocking is that the sentence in
progress finishes.

**Mutations are deliberately still blocking.** A model that narrates
"done" while the operation is in flight will eventually say it about one
that failed. The pause on a write is the write landing, and it is worth
hearing.

### Voice can read what it is allowed to change (#337)

`c9fef3e`

Dion, after selecting a node and asking whether voice could see it: "It
seemed confused." It was right to be. The session could `selection_set`
and `selection_clear` and had NO way to read a selection, so "can you
see what I've selected?" was a question it could only guess at — and
guessing is what a model does.

The selection is the cheapest thing a person does to say "this one", and
it was the one fact the projection left out. Rows now carry SELECTED and
the header explains it — but only when something IS selected, because a
line describing a marker that appears nowhere teaches the model to look
for it and find nothing, which is how "the selected one" becomes a guess
a second way.

It travels through `ComposerFacts` like `theme` and `groupMode` before
it: selection is the shell's UI state, not canvas state, and a module
that read the shell's stores would stop being removable. Absent rather
than false where a surface has none — the standing harness has no
viewport and no pointer, and `false` from it would claim nothing is
selected rather than that it cannot tell.

**Two more of the same shape, found by looking for it.**

`read_canvas` in the browser answered with a shorter list of its own —
titles and ids — so a model that re-read the canvas got LESS than it was
told at setup: no geometry, no colour, no selection. Re-reading to check
something is exactly when the facts must not get thinner. It answers in
the projection's one wording now.

`read_item` answered with the title and the id, both of which the asker
already had, because that is how it addressed the item. So "tell me
about this one" returned nothing at all. It now answers with the item's
own projection row plus what a single item can say and a list cannot:
the description, the current file, how many versions, the reactions.

The audit behind this is 33 write tools against 10 read tools, and the
