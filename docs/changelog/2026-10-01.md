# 1 October 2026 — What needs a person

The cleanup walk closed on 27 September with nothing scheduled behind it, and
the question "what's next?" turned up an answer no commit could give: design
competition phase 0, a bout judged by three people, had sat on `main` for
seventeen days waiting for one afternoon. It was not hiding. It was simply
not anywhere a reader looks first.

## The roadmap opens with what needs a person

`docs/verify/` has been the queue of things built, shipped and never exercised
by a human since 20 September. But its table was hand-kept in its own README,
a copy of what each walk's `**Status:**` line already said, and the roadmap,
where people go to ask what's left, did not show it at all. The same kind of
second copy the roadmap was built to get rid of.

Each walk now says its own state in front matter: `status`, `since`, `never`
(what has never been exercised), `needs` (what the person needs in hand) and,
when there is one, `issue`. Core's `verifyStatus` reads it through the same
front-matter reader as `docStatus`. `scripts/roadmap.mjs` puts the result
first on `docs/ROADMAP.md`, ahead of everything waiting on somebody building,
because this is the one list waiting on somebody *using*. The README's table
is now a pointer. A walk that leaves out what was never exercised, what you
need, or a date fails the roadmap's check, and so does a walk marked `broken`
with no issue: a person handed a walk should not have to guess.

## Six walks that phases owed but nobody had written

A phase that says "the human walk remains" adds nothing to any list. So the
walks those phases owed are now written:

- design competition phase 0: can three people tell whose entry is whose (#257)
- a real bout from the Chat to the winner, on dev (phases 3–5.5)
- bring your own fighter, from a teammate's git repo (phase 6)
- mobile on a physical phone: the keyboard, Safari's toolbar, how gestures feel
- a real `/sprint` with people who have not read the design (sprint phase 6)
- the operator's six acts on the hosted home, as a doorway to its existing
  runbook rather than a copy of it

The roadmap now lists eleven. Design competition phase 8's container-image
removal walk is not among them: it needs Docker, not a person, and an agent
can run it.
