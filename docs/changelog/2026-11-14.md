### exact phase 7: ship

`ad798d9`

An Exact app now has a release lane on the canvas, an exact-lane item built
from app.json's deploy table: channels as rows, each release as a card
linking the screens it changed, with before and after versions.

Prepare (`isocan exact prepare`, or the lane's button, through both doors)
runs Exact's own dry run, `deploy.mjs --json`. It hashes the origin directory
either side and posts the classification as a card: what goes over the air
and what needs a binary, sorted by Exact's `action` with Exact's reason.
Agents may prepare. When no directory was hashed, the card says so instead of
claiming nothing was uploaded; that was caught in verification.

Ship, Promote and Roll back belong to a person. The CLI refuses when its
actor is an enrolled agent, and `isocan exact live` refuses a canvas-door ask
whose writer, read off the op log, is an agent. Both use one sentence. The
CLI prints the channel, change count and classification and takes `--yes`;
the lane opens a confirm step. Ship runs `deploy.mjs --yes` pinned to the
prepared commit, signed with keys that stay on the machine. Roll back
re-publishes the earlier commit's content from a worktree at a new sequence
number, and the card says it is a roll back.

The proof was re-run by the conductor on the scratch home:

  is unchanged.
- A person's CLI ship of a text change reached seq 5. Acme Market, running on

  6.3 s after the head was written; the check interval is 10 s.
- Roll back reached seq 6, and the app fetched and showed the earlier text
  within 4 s.

The builder walked these in the browser too: an agent prepared through a real
claude-code turn, a person shipped and promoted to production, and Prepare
named a grant change as needs-a-binary with the origin unchanged.

Deep suite: 9,518 passed. Typecheck clean.

Course changes:
- Exact counts git-ignored files as uncommitted, so isocan refuses tracked
  changes, passes `--dirty` and pins the commit itself.
- A channel is the URL clients bake in, while Exact writes to the directory
  the person names (ISOCAN_EXACT_ORIGIN).
- Exact can call one change both over the air and needs-a-binary; the card
  shows both.

Open:

### exact phase 6: agents propose

`1aa52c6`

Agents change an Exact app only by proposing. An enrolled agent pairs with
Tamber at `propose` scope; anyone else the canvas lets write gets `write`.
`isocan exact propose <screen>` (token edits, `set-row` style edits, raw
ops) only writes `exact.propose` on the screen. `isocan exact live` holds
the proposal on the dev loop's wire and pictures it before and after on
web phone, tablet and desktop in light and dark and on iOS. Fact 3 holds:
a held proposal does not render, so live-class changes are pictured as a
scrub then scrub-cancel, and rebuild-class changes in a staging copy of
the app. The pictures become versions of a core proposal item under the
screen. Keep is core's choose, which live turns into Tamber's accept; an
agent's own accept is refused by the wire (403, needs write).

Directions use design-competition. On a bout whose target carries
`exact.route`, the fighter rule tells fighters to propose with `isocan
exact propose ... --in <lane>`, and competition's Take it refuses in
favour of Keep, so a winner never lands as a picture without its accept.

The design-system item's token chips scrub every live frame: the web
loop's and a native iOS frame on the bridge's own simulator. `isocan exact
branch <app> <ref>` stages a git ref with `git archive` and captures its
screens beside the current ones, new routes in a row of their own.
Captures now build into their own EXACT_WEB_DIST, because a web-build in
the app directory left the loop's pages ignoring Tamber.

### exact phase 5: tests are storyboards

as Drive, Film and Inspect: the bridge's /run when paired, or `exact.run`
on the storyboard answered by `isocan exact live` with Live frames off.
`isocan exact run` does it from the CLI.

### exact phase 4: time and state

`35bbcb8`

Motion in an Exact app can now be looked at frame by frame. `isocan
exact film <app> <route> --do "tap add-to-cart" --over 600 --every 16`,
or Film on a screen, runs Exact's own `screenshot ... apng over 600
every 16` after the press. The strip lands as an .apng image item
carrying `exact.film`. The browser splits it into stills, because an
<img> cannot be seeked, and draws a scrubber in milliseconds that shows
the frame drawn at or before the moment, with Play. Filming again with
the same inputs gives the same bytes, so no new version is posted.

State per frame comes from a second cold driver stepping `clock +16`
with `state` and `layout <watch>` between steps. The clock only moves
forward, so a film cannot be followed by reading its frames' state.
Both drivers are deterministic, so the instants agree. The state is
kept as the property `exact.frames` (first frame whole, then the
changes), out of gc's reach, and shown under the scrubber.

Comments at a moment reuse core's `Comment.at`, motion's shape. Opening
one scrubs the strip back to it, through the shell's
`WebModule.comments` call: a module is never told a thread is open, so
a comment shown again after 1.5 s idle counts as reopened. The
inspector panel shows `tree --ax`, layout, perf and logs for the
selected screen, fresh from a cold driver at the screen's shots and
never the stored points. Film and inspect use the same two doors as
Drive: the paired bridge, or with Live frames off a request property
(`exact.filmAsk`, `exact.inspect`) answered by `isocan exact live`.

The fixture's place page gained a cart sheet whose entrance is
`transition="translate -exact-spring(300, 22, 1)"`. It overshoots to
-27 px at 240 ms and settles by 592 ms.

Proof, re-run by the conductor on a scratch home:

  frames), the same bytes as the builder's run.
- Film pressed on the canvas with Live frames off produced those same
  bytes, with 0 requests to the machine's other loopback ports.
- A comment at 180 ms, after scrubbing away to 500, scrubbed the strip
  back to frame 12 (drawn at 176 ms). Six scrub points showed six

- Deep run: 9,488 passed. Typecheck 0.

composited outside the clock), so films are web-only; comment pins at

### exact phase 0: the bridge, the index, and the three doors
