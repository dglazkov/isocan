# 9 September 2026 — The home peek floats, and fits the window it opens in

Hovering a canvas card on the home screen grew the card
by the preview's height and pushed the whole grid down — the page jumped
under the pointer, measured at 186px of neighbour shift. The peek had been
deliberately placed in flow ("a popover floating outside would need placing,
and this is a few short rows that the card has room for"), and that
reasoning was reversed the moment it was measured: the peek is now
absolutely positioned, hung off the card's bottom edge over its neighbours,
on the menu's opaque ground, with the card's footprint fixed so the grid
never moves. The DOM position stayed put, so Tab still walks from the open
link through the peek's rows to the ··· row; Escape dismisses, and the
pointer leaving cannot take a preview the keyboard is still reading.

A card near the bottom edge opens its preview upward — but the first cut
chose the side and then applied the stylesheet's fixed 240px cap, which is a
guess about the window. On a short one (measured at 800×300: card 98–213)
the upward peek ran from −94 to 99 — 94px of it above the viewport,
unreadable. The cap is now the smaller of the stylesheet's 240 and the
actual room on the chosen side, less an 8px breath, computed by one pure
function (`peekplace.ts`) the component calls on open and on resize — and
the executing cases in `peekplace.test.ts` replay the measured failure. The
preview scrolls what does not fit, and Tab reaches its links without the
page moving.

Live feedback caught a second visual edge: the card still ended in rounded
corners above a separately bordered preview. The open card and preview now
share the same opaque background and border colour. Their joining corners
are square, the preview covers the card's existing border at the join, and
neither half casts a shadow across the other. The card keeps its border
widths so its footprint does not change. This also works in reverse when
the preview opens upward; cards without a rendered preview stay unchanged.
Verified in a real browser in light and dark themes, at 800×300 and at a
390px width, including pointer entry, Tab, Escape, internal scrolling,
resize while open, and a real click through to an item.

No op, CLI verb, agent-guide, shared-core or README change: this only changes
how existing information is presented on hover or focus.

## The peek reads as a thing in front, and the ordering reads as one control

The overlay landed flat. A peek covers most of the card beneath it but stops
about seventeen pixels short of that card's bottom edge, so a sliver of the
covered card poked out underneath with nothing anywhere on screen saying
which of the two was in front. Reported as "the middle card overlays the card
below" — read, correctly, as a rendering fault rather than as a preview.

The hovered or focused card now lifts: a shadow, and 2.5% of scale, so it is
nearer as well as brighter. The shadow is drawn by `filter`, not `box-shadow`,
because the card and the preview hanging off it are two boxes making one
surface and two box-shadows draw a line across the join — which is why the
peek had turned shadows off on both halves in the first place. A `filter`
shadow follows the rendered silhouette, so one shadow wraps the pair and the
join stays invisible. A transform is paint, never layout, so the neighbours
still do not move a pixel: measured open and closed, every card's `offsetTop`,
`offsetLeft` and `offsetHeight` are identical. Focus lifts as well as hover,
since focus is the other way a peek opens; the create card is excluded, or it
would push toward you while you typed a name into it. Reduced motion keeps the
shadow and drops the approach — depth is not movement.

`drop-shadow()` has no spread, which is the whole reason the offset is small:
the shadow the pop token can afford (`0 14px 30px -14px`) drew a full-size grey
copy of the card sticking out below it, an offset slab rather than a halo.

The preview's cap moved from 240 to 288. Five seams is what `useCardPeek`
asks for, and a seam that names an item draws a thumbnail: a linked row
measures 51px against a plain row's 18. Five of those need 282, so on any
canvas whose recent seams were about items the last row was sliced — and with
overlay scrollbars there is no visible track and no fade, so it read as broken
rather than as scrollable. The stylesheet's `max-height` is now asserted equal
to `PEEK_CAP`: two files held one measurement with nothing tying them
together, which is how they came to disagree.

The placement also moved from `useEffect` to `useLayoutEffect`. `useEffect`
runs after paint, so the first frame carried the previous placement — side
down, full cap — and the correction was a visible jump: 353px at 900×700, and
at 800×240 a box hanging 134px below the fold before it clamped to 97. A box
that moves after you see it is the bug this component exists to stop, wearing
a different hat.

Separately, the canvas list's ordering became a segmented control. Recent
activity, Name and Newest first were three chips two pixels apart, which said
"three separate things you can press" and left the set — which is the meaning —
undrawn. They now sit in a track, with the chosen one standing proud of it.
Not a new look: the theme switch has been that control since it was written,
so both wear a shared `segmented` class rather than growing a second
segmented style. `Archived` moved out of the track, because it widens what is
listed rather than choosing how it is ordered, and a fourth segment in a
one-of-N groove would have promised that turning the shelf on turns an
ordering off — which also makes the `role="group"` labelled `Order` true of
everything inside it for the first time.
