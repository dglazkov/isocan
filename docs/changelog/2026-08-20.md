# Pointing at things — and a cloud era that was wound back

**2026-08-20** · 20 commits · 103 files · +5,067 · Dimitri Glazkov, Dion Almaer

Annotation, attachments, favourites and real thumbnails on one side; on the other, a whole multiuser era built on a designated host and a Firestore mirror — which shipped and was reverted the next day.

## Ink that is about something

- **Annotation**: scribble over a screen and the ink carries `annotates=<item>` and `region=x,y,w,h` as FRACTIONS of the target, so an agent can act on "the right-hand block of the upper half" without parsing strokes, and it survives a resize.
- The mark paints above what it marks and travels with it — in the web app's drag and in `isocan mv`, through one shared helper.
- Clearing is asymmetric on purpose: ink about an item asked for something; ink on bare canvas is not the agent's to tidy.

## The message knows what it is about

- Selected items ride along as chips over the composer and post as ids on the comment. The chips ARE the selection — removing one deselects it.
- **Favourites**: a star as a property on the item, so it survives a reload, appears on the other machine, and agents can read `ls --starred`.
- Thumbnails render the item's own content everywhere a peek happens; ⌘⏎ sends.

## The era that was wound back

- `isocan share` provisioned a canvas's own Firebase project end to end — Firestore, credentials, canned rules that let nobody in, Hosting — in about ninety seconds, resumable at every step.
- The host daemon became the sole writer of a shared canvas's mirror, hanging off the engine's op event rather than its write chain so an outage could never sit between a person and their canvas.
- All of it was reverted the following day: "the designated-host + Firestore-mirror approach is off the table."
